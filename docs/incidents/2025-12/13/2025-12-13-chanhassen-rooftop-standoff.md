# Construction Workers Take Refuge on Roof During Hours-Long ICE Standoff

## Updates
- **Feb 8** - Major enrichment from CBS News, Carver County Local News, EP Local News. Added full timeline, witness accounts, official statements, community response details, DHS statements. Added 5 new sources. Upgraded trustworthiness to HIGH.

## Summary
Two construction workers and brothers, Edgar and Marco Chicaiza Dutan, took refuge on an unfinished roof frame at a townhome construction site in the Avienda development in Chanhassen as approximately 10-15 ICE agents surrounded them. The standoff lasted roughly four hours in temperatures of negative four degrees with steady winds of 10-20 mph. Agents denied community members' requests to provide hand warmers or blankets to the men on the roof. As the crowd grew to approximately 100 people — many of whom had completed local bystander training — and news crews arrived, ICE eventually departed. Edgar came down first and was hospitalized at Ridgeview Hospital before being transferred to ICE custody at Freeborn County Adult Detention Center. Marco remained in the rafters until after agents left. Both were hospitalized in satisfactory condition. State Rep. Alex Falconer reported that agents lacked a judicial warrant and "didn't know who they were trying to find." The Carver County Sheriff's Office had not been given prior notice of the operation.

## Timeline
- **Morning (~10:00 AM)** - Approximately 10-15 ICE agents appear at a townhome construction site in the Avienda development in Chanhassen. Two brothers, Edgar and Marco Chicaiza Dutan, are working on the roof frame.
- Agents describe the initial contact as a "consensual encounter." They claim the men fled when asked for identification, providing probable cause for pursuit. An agent tells a witness the area was targeted due to a known criminal but agents were uncertain if either man on the roof was that person.
- The brothers move onto the unfinished roof frame rather than come down. Agents surround the building.
- **~11:00 AM** - Community members begin arriving after word spreads through social media and local rapid-response networks. Only the front of the property is taped off.
- Elise Boll, a Carver County resident who had attended local "upstander" trainings, grabs hand and foot warmers from her car and walks toward the back of the building. An agent stops her: "You cannot throw those." The clearly labeled hand warmer package is not allowed through.
- Community members request permission to provide blankets and warm items to the men on the roof. Agents deny all requests.
- Carver County Sheriff's deputies arrive at the scene. They had not been given prior notice of the ICE operation. Their role is traffic control and monitoring, not assisting ICE.
- **By early afternoon** - The crowd grows to approximately 100 people. Temperatures are negative four degrees with wind chill much colder. One of the men on the roof is described as "so still, face away from the cold."
- **~1:00 PM** - The crowd grows more tense. News crews begin arriving and filming.
- Edgar comes down from the roof. He is taken by ambulance to Ridgeview Hospital before being transferred to ICE custody.
- Two women bring blankets and supplies to the scene. Shortly after news crews arrive and begin filming, ICE departs.
- **~1:30 PM** - Agents leave the area. Marco remains in the rafters and comes down after agents depart. He is later taken to a hospital.

## Witness Accounts

### Elise Boll (Carver County Resident)
Boll had attended local "upstander" trainings on how to safely document ICE activity. She arrived around 11:00 AM.

"I walked to the back of the house and an agent walked up and stopped me, and he immediately said, 'You cannot throw those.' He said, 'No, you are not allowed to throw anything.'"

"The hand warmer package was clearly labeled. And what bothers me is that in the media, they are showing people giving the man blankets and all sorts of stuff. But that didn't happen until several hours into the situation, when the news crews started showing up and filming."

### Christina (Chanhassen Resident, First Name Only)
Arrived late morning as the crowd grew.

"The crowd was determined and on fire. Everyone was angry and sad. As in Minnesota fashion, people brought things to share, such as hand warmers, food and coffee. Some people came, like me, ready for the cold. Some people were not dressed for it, but refused to leave."

On the man remaining on the roof: "He was so still, face away from the cold. ICE surrounded the building and would not allow anyone near. ICE was torturing him with the arctic air."

"Those men in the Chanhassen event were tortured. They were given a choice, either be detained by ICE or freeze."

### Miranda (Witness, CBS News)
"It was scary, it was really really scary. I guess it was something you never think is going to happen."

### Alex Trevino (Witness, CBS News)
"It was probably two below zero and they were building a home."

## Injuries
- **Edgar Chicaiza Dutan:** Hospitalized at Ridgeview Hospital after prolonged cold exposure; reported in satisfactory condition.
- **Marco Chicaiza Dutan:** Hospitalized after prolonged cold exposure; reported in satisfactory condition.

## Community Response
The incident was notable for the scale and organization of the community response in a suburban setting. Many of those who arrived had previously participated in local bystander and "upstander" trainings organized by regional advocacy groups, which teach volunteers how to safely observe law enforcement activity, document what they see, and connect affected families with legal and social support.

The crowd grew from a few dozen people to approximately 100 over the course of the morning, despite temperatures well below zero. Residents brought hand warmers, food, and coffee. The Carver County Sheriff's Office, which had no prior notice of the operation, sent deputies for traffic control and community safety — not to assist ICE.

## Context
This incident was significant as one of the first major ICE enforcement actions in a suburban Twin Cities community during Operation Metro Surge. Previous confrontations had been concentrated in Minneapolis neighborhoods. The organized community response — with trained bystanders arriving within an hour — demonstrated the spread of rapid-response networks beyond the urban core. Chanhassen is a predominantly white, affluent suburb in Carver County.
