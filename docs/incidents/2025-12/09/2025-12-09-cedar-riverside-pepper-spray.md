# ICE Agents Pepper Spray Observers and Protesters at Cedar-Riverside

## Summary
As ICE agents attempted to leave the Cedar-Riverside neighborhood after detaining multiple people — including [U.S. citizen Mubashir](/entry/2025-12-09-mubashir-wrongful-detention) — agents repeatedly pepper sprayed observers and protesters from their vehicle windows without warning. No one was blocking or impeding the vehicles, which had a clear roadway to leave. Alan Crenshaw, a 35-year-old University of Minnesota student, was sprayed directly in the face while standing in a crosswalk. Multiple witnesses described agents stopping cars that were already pulling away to get out and spray people standing on sidewalks. Snow across the area was stained orange from the volume of pepper spray used.

## Timeline
- **Afternoon** - Crenshaw hears ICE is in the Cedar Riverside area. He walks from campus to observe and document.
- Someone tells him ICE agents just went inside Sagal Restaurant and Coffee at 326 Cedar Ave.
- **Inside Sagal Restaurant** - Crenshaw arrives as two agents are violently slamming a young Black man (Mubashir) against the wall. The man is yelling in pain and saying he is a U.S. citizen. The agents ignore him. They drag him outside. He is not resisting.
- **Outside** - Agents push the man into the snow, handcuff him, and put him in a car (black windows, Florida plate QFP1709). An agent slams the door on observers. People from the restaurant bring pictures of the man's ID and hold them up to the car window. Agents ignore them.
- **Departure and first spray** - The car pulls away. Observers follow on foot, recording, blowing whistles, yelling. No one is getting in the way of the cars, which are moving slowly. As they leave, a window rolls down and an arm comes out and pepper sprays the crowd with no warning. Crenshaw is about 10-15 feet away. The snow where they were standing is stained bright orange.
- **6th Street, west of Cedar Ave** - Crenshaw moves to another group observing. About nine ICE vehicles with lights on are attempting to leave a parking lot, causing a 20-minute traffic jam. During this time, observers record, chant, blow whistles, and tell ICE to leave.
- **Multiple spraying incidents** - A person in a brown coat and black hat is standing on the edge of the road (not in the road). A black SUV drives past, slows, and opens its door. The person holds their arms out to their sides. An agent gets out and sprays them directly. The person moves away. Another agent on foot comes from behind and sprays them directly in the face again. Agents then spray into the small crowd. This is gratuitous — agents could have left the scene instead.
- **Crenshaw sprayed** - Standing in the crosswalk on the side of the road, another ICE car without its lights on drives past and sprays him directly in the face. Immediate intense pain. He cannot keep his eyes open or breathe. Hard coughing. Red swelling and spots on his eyes for approximately 24 hours. Skin tingling for several hours. When he showers later, the pepper spray runs back into his eyes.

## Witness Account: Joe Mitchell

Mitchell, a Cedar Riverside attorney who lived two blocks from the scene, arrived at the intersection of Cedar Avenue and 6th Street at approximately 2:00 PM after his spouse alerted him to ICE activity via a neighborhood group chat. He walked over (he cannot run due to ankle problems) and positioned himself on the north sidewalk of 6th Street.

Mitchell observed a female agent (brown-skinned) in the passenger seat of an ICE vehicle repeatedly display and shake a canister of chemical irritant out her window at protesters and observers who were not immediately surrounding the vehicle. Her display of the canister "aggravated the protesters into being louder." One protester walked toward her but did not get close enough to be within reach. The car had plenty of space to pull away — nobody was blocking it.

Eventually the driver pulled the car away. As it started to move, the passenger agent displayed the irritant again. The car pulled ahead approximately 20 feet and had a clear lane to continue driving. Instead of continuing, agents stopped the car, got out, and sprayed pepper spray behind them at the crowd of protesters who had followed. At the time the spray was deployed, the protesters were not within reach of the car, and the car had room to continue driving.

Mitchell saw numerous protesters with orange caking their faces. A broad swath of snow was stained orange. Though several rows back and not close to the deployment, Mitchell was left doubling over coughing. He experienced tingling on his knuckles, fingertips, eyes, and lips for the rest of the day despite repeated washing. He also experienced digestive trouble he could not attribute to any other cause.

Mitchell states: "I did not see anything that, in my judgment, exposed the ICE agents to any risk of harm."

## Witness Account: Mary Hackman

Hackman, 66, has lived in the Cedar Riverside neighborhood since 1992. She heard about ICE vehicles in the area from her neighborhood chat and headed out, arriving at the northwest corner of 6th and Cedar Avenue. She was blowing a whistle to alert the community and as protest.

Hackman watched ICE vehicles trying to get through a traffic jam. One vehicle angled to turn south onto Cedar Avenue. Standing on the sidewalk with a few other people, facing the passenger side of the car, Hackman saw the front passenger window come down. A female agent wearing a neck gaiter pointed a pepper spray canister at them.

Shortly afterward, the car moved several feet forward and stopped alongside people standing on the edge of Cedar Avenue — likely on the sidewalk, though it was difficult to tell with the mound of snow. Both the female agent and the male driver jumped out and sprayed the people standing there without warning. The people they sprayed were not blocking any vehicles. They were standing, recording, chanting, shouting, and blowing whistles.

The spray drifted toward Hackman and she felt it as she inhaled, then turned away. When she went back to assist the people who had been sprayed, their eyes were swollen and red.

Hackman states: "It all happened relatively quickly, but my impression was that spraying the people was punitive, a way to get back at them for observing."

On continuing: "It breaks my heart to see my neighbors in fear... they have only made the neighborhood stronger and more vibrant."

## Legal Action

### Tincher v. Noem (Case 0:25-cv-04669-KMM-DTS)
Crenshaw is a named plaintiff in ACLU of Minnesota lawsuit filed December 17, 2025. The suit seeks a preliminary injunction limiting agents' use of firearms, chemical irritants, and arrests of citizens not resisting dispersal orders.

### January 17 Preliminary Injunction
U.S. District Judge Katherine Menendez partially granted the injunction, prohibiting federal agents from retaliating against peaceful protesters, using pepper spray against peaceful protesters in retaliation, arresting or detaining protesters without probable cause, and stopping vehicles solely for safely following agents. Defendants were required to distribute the injunction to all agents within 72 hours.

## Context
The pepper spraying occurred as agents were leaving — not while they were under threat or being impeded. The ACLU complaint emphasizes this point repeatedly: agents "had a clear roadway to drive away from the area if they wanted to do so." The complaint characterizes the use of chemical irritants as "gratuitous" retaliation against observers exercising First Amendment rights. This pattern — agents spraying from vehicle windows as they departed — was cited as a key factor in the January 17 preliminary injunction.
