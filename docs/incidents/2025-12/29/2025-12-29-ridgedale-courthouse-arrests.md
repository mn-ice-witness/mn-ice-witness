# Dozens of Armed ICE Agents Arrest Two Men at Ridgedale Library and Courthouse; Observer Shoved Twice

## Summary
Approximately 30 armed, masked ICE agents descended on the Ridgedale Service Center in Minnetonka — a building housing a public library, courtrooms, a DMV, a coffee shop, and a bookstore — to arrest two men outside the Fourth Judicial District courtrooms. The men had apparently come to the building for court appearances. Carla Hennes, a 54-year-old Minnetonka resident who had responded to a community alert to check on children whose parents might have been taken, was assaulted twice by agents: first grabbed by the back of the head and shoved so hard her glasses flew off while standing on a staircase inside the building, then shoved from behind on the sidewalk outside while an agent said "Stop touching me" despite no contact. About 15 armed agents escorted each individual man out of the building. Two Hennepin County sheriff's deputies observed from outside; after witnessing an agent shove Hennes, one deputy asked the agents if they needed help.

## Affected Individual(s)

### Carla Hennes (Observer, Declarant)
- **Age:** 54
- **Residence:** Glen Lake neighborhood, Minnetonka (25+ years)
- **Occupation:** Software quality assurance
- **Role:** Community observer responding to rapid-response group chat alert

### Two Unnamed Men (Arrested)
- **Details:** Two men arrested outside the Fourth Judicial District courtrooms. Per Hennes's account, at least one had apparently come for a court appearance — "one man who had come to court, presumably doing what he was supposed to do."
- **Status:** Taken into ICE custody

## Timeline
- **Morning** - Hennes receives a message in a community group chat that ICE has been spotted at the library and county service center at 12601 Ridgedale Drive in Minnetonka. People are asked to check if children whose parents may have been taken need assistance. She heads over.
- **Arrival** - Hennes spots a white Wagoneer she recognizes as an ICE vehicle — it had previously been seen leaving the Whipple Building and at the Home2Suites on Highway 62 and 494 where ICE agents are staying.
- She walks through the building. First floor: public meeting rooms. Second floor: public library, coffee shop, bookstore, DMV. Third floor: security screening, courtrooms, waiting area.
- On the second floor she talks to a man who says people he thinks are ICE are in the building. She goes to the coffee shop, orders a coffee, and lets her community group know there are likely ICE agents inside. She asks for more observers to join her.
- **After noon** - Hennes and other observers who have joined see two men walk up the stairs to the courtroom area. One is wearing two gun holsters under his jacket. They go through the metal detector, which goes off, but guards nod them through after seeing ID.
- **Agents flood the building** - Hennes goes back to the first floor to watch the elevator. Agents start pouring into the building. They are masked, in flak jackets mostly marked "ICE," armed with guns, tasers, and chemical irritant weapons. She starts blowing her whistle and alerts the group chat.
- She follows agents upstairs. Near the top of the stairs at the third level, a library security guard blocks her and tells her to stop blowing her whistle.
- **First assault** - While blocked on the staircase by the security guard, an ICE agent comes up, grabs Hennes by the back of the head, and shoves her so hard her glasses come off. He says nothing before or after. She was not in his way and there was plenty of room on the steps. She goes back downstairs.
- More agents continue arriving, masked and geared up. She blows her whistle again. Two Hennepin County sheriff's deputies stand outside, observing.
- **First arrest (~15 minutes later)** - About 15 armed agents bring one man in handcuffs down the stairs.
- Hennes follows them outside to try to get the man's name and tells agents they could get a better job.
- **Second assault** - Walking down the sidewalk outside, an agent comes up behind Hennes and shoves her. As he shoves her, he says "Stop touching me." She was not touching him.
- A Hennepin County sheriff's deputy, who may have just witnessed the shove, leans over to the agents and asks if they need help.
- **Second arrest** - Hennes goes back inside and waits. Agents eventually bring another man in handcuffs downstairs, again with approximately 15 armed agents escorting one person.

## Witness Account: Carla Hennes

On the scale of the operation: "If I had to guess, there were 30 plus agents there total that day, but I didn't stop to count them."

On being blocked by library security: "Apparently, I was causing more of a disturbance than 30 armed agents in a library where children were."

On the second shove outside: "As he shoved me, he said 'Stop touching me.' I stopped short, in disbelief."

On the sheriff's deputy's response: "When the agents were coming back after putting the man in one of their cars, one of the Hennepin Sheriff's deputies leaned over to them and asked if they needed help. They may have just seen an ICE agent shove me for no reason and still asked them if they needed help."

On why she observes: "It's my hope that me being there did some good. All of us observing is a way to provide some sunshine on their misbehavior. I wouldn't ever try to interfere — we know we can't stop them, but we can help build a case to expose their cruelty and to stop unlawful practices."

On the proportionality: "It's like we're living in a banana republic with these commandos in libraries where kids go to play and learn. Just to arrest two men who were there at court. It's not like they were hardened criminals, at least as far as I know."

On the aftermath: "This really shook me. Two armed federal law enforcement agents pushed me for nothing more than observing them and letting them know I disagree with what they're doing. I managed on adrenaline while I was there, but when I got home, I collapsed, shaking, trembling, and crying. My kids told me they'd never seen me distressed like that before. I was too upset to go out to observe the following day, but I'm determined to keep protecting my neighbors and expressing my dislike for what the government is doing in my name."

## Injuries
- **Carla Hennes:** Grabbed by the back of the head and shoved inside the building (glasses knocked off). Shoved from behind on the sidewalk outside. Severe emotional distress afterward — collapsed at home shaking, trembling, and crying.

## Official Statements

### Hennepin County
"This afternoon more than a dozen ICE agents were in the public waiting area outside the Fourth Judicial District courtrooms at the Ridgedale Service Center. These agents arrested two individuals."

The county stated that public areas of its facilities are accessible to law enforcement performing official duties, but non-public areas such as employee workspaces or secure areas require proper authorization or a warrant.

"We expect all agencies operating in our facilities to conduct themselves in a manner that respects the rights and dignity of individuals and that recognizes the important interests of Hennepin County in providing services to its residents."

## Legal Action

### Tincher v. Noem (Case 0:25-cv-04669-KMM-DTS)
Hennes's declaration was filed December 31, 2025, as Document 33 in the ACLU of Minnesota lawsuit challenging ICE's treatment of observers and protesters during Operation Metro Surge. While not a named plaintiff, her account supports the pattern of agents using force against peaceful observers.

## Context
The operation at Ridgedale was notable for the extreme disproportion of force — approximately 30 armed, masked agents deployed to a public building housing a library, coffee shop, and DMV to arrest two men who were apparently complying with court obligations. The building's security guards let armed agents through the metal detector without question while a library security guard told a woman blowing a whistle that she was causing a disturbance. The presence of ICE agents at courthouses has been widely criticized as deterring immigrants from attending court hearings, undermining the judicial process.
