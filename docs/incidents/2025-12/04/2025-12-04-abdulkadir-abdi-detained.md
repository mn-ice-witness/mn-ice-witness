# Addiction Recovery Leader Detained, Labeled "Gang Member" — Later Released by Federal Judge

## Updates
- **2026-02-08**: Added Star Tribune "worst of the worst" investigation details. Updated status: federal judge in Nebraska ordered release after government withdrew opposition; ICE barred from detaining him again. Added court filing source. Changed status to resolved, trustworthiness to high.

## Summary
Abdulkadir Sharif Abdi, 47, a Somali refugee and well-known addiction recovery counselor, was detained by ICE on December 1, 2025, at his Minneapolis home. Federal officials alleged he was a current gang member and said they had "orders" and a warrant for his arrest. ICE labeled him among the "worst of the worst" on the DHS website. However, Abdi's last conviction was over a decade ago, and in 2018 a federal judge noted he had "worked to turn his life around" since 2007. Just before Christmas, government attorneys withdrew their opposition to his release. A federal judge in Nebraska barred ICE from detaining him again. Despite his release, Abdi remains featured on the DHS "worst of the worst" website. A Star Tribune investigation of the 240 names DHS released found that while 80% had felony convictions, nearly all had already served their time and were not actively wanted by police.

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Name:** Abdulkadir Sharif Abdi
- **Age:** 47
- **Arrival:** Came to U.S. in 1996 as Somali refugee fleeing civil war
- **Occupation:** Works at homeless shelter helping Indigenous population; formerly worked at Park Avenue Center substance abuse facility
- **Recovery:** Sober for 13 years, leadership position in Alcoholics Anonymous

## Background
After arriving as a refugee, Abdi became involved with gangs and committed felonies including fraud, receiving stolen property, and vehicle theft. However, around 2007 he began turning his life around.

## 2018 Case
In 2018, during Trump's first term, Abdi was targeted for deportation. A U.S. Magistrate noted:
- He had "worked to turn his life around" since about 2007.
- "He has worked with law enforcement and the community to discourage young Somali-Americans from associating with gangs and extremist groups."
- He had "joined Alcoholics Anonymous and was recently elected to a leadership position."
- Since 2014, worked at substance abuse treatment facility.

In February 2019, Judge Sarah B. Mazzie released him on his own recognizance after community rallied to save him.

## ICE's Characterization
DHS described Abdi as "a former member of the Gangster Disciples and is a known current member of Vice Lord Nation" with multiple felony convictions.

## Wife's Response
Christenson pushed back, saying Abdi is NOT a current gang member and had turned his life around after getting sober 13 years ago.

## Current Status
Initially processed at Whipple Federal Building at Fort Snelling. Transferred to detention facility in Nebraska. Just before Christmas 2025, government attorneys withdrew their opposition to his release. A federal judge in Nebraska barred ICE from detaining him again. Abdi is free but remains listed on the DHS "worst of the worst" website.

## Star Tribune Investigation
A Star Tribune investigation of the 240 names DHS released on the "worst of the worst" list found:
- About 80% had felony convictions for crimes like murder, rape, theft, drugs, or fraud
- Nearly everyone had already gone through the court system, served their time, and was not actively wanted by police
- More than a quarter had no criminal connection to Minnesota except being held in a federal prison in the state
- Little to no criminal histories for more than a dozen cases aside from traffic tickets, gross misdemeanors, and DWIs
- The list represents about 8% of the 3,000 people ICE claims to have detained in Minnesota

## Editorial Assessment
**HIGH** - Multiple news sources (CBS, KARE 11, Racket MN) confirm original detention and background. Star Tribune investigative report provides broader context on the "worst of the worst" list. Federal court records confirm release ordered by judge in Nebraska. The arc of this case — a man whose rehabilitation was recognized by a federal judge in 2018, re-detained, labeled a current gang member, then released again when the government withdrew its own opposition — illustrates the gap between the administration's "worst of the worst" framing and the reality of who is being detained.
