# Elderly Couple Has Guns Pointed at Them After Following ICE Vehicle

## Summary
John Biestman and Janet Lee, a married couple in their late 60s from Linden Hills, had guns pointed at them and were threatened with arrest after following an ICE vehicle from a raid at Church of the Assumption of the Blessed Mary in Richfield.

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Names:** John Biestman and Janet Lee
- **Ages:** 69 (Biestman), 67 (Lee)
- **Citizenship:** U.S. Citizens
- **Residence:** Linden Hills, Minneapolis
- **Occupations:** Biestman is a retired banker; Lee is a speech-language pathologist (39 years)

## Timeline
- **~11:00 AM** - Approximately eight observers gather at Church of the Assumption after Richfield Indivisible posts on Facebook asking community members to observe and record. A black Ford Explorer (plate 841597) is spotted driving erratically through the neighborhood.
- **~11:26 AM** - Biestman and Lee arrive at the church after hearing ICE vehicles were circling it as Sunday morning services were finishing.
- ICE vehicles spotted speeding through side streets adjacent to the church, running red lights, driving recklessly.
- Biestman and Lee begin following what they believe is an ICE vehicle, driving carefully and lawfully.
- They turn into Roosevelt Park near the church.
- **Confrontation** - Four unmarked ICE vehicles box in their car. Masked agents with no visible identification surround them. Agents point semiautomatic weapons at the couple at close range and demand they roll down their windows.
- Biestman tells agents: "What you're doing is illegal, this is like Germany 1938."
- One agent reaches through the driver's side window, points at Janet Lee: "We're going to arrest her too, we have handcuffs."
- Biestman asks if they have a warrant. They say they don't need one.
- A female agent asks: "Don't you care about sexual predators?" — claiming that's what they were arresting.
- Agents continue threatening, pointing guns in both their faces, mocking them. Several agents video the encounter, including their faces and license plate.
- An agent tells them: "We have your license plate, we now know where to find you."
- Lee wants to record the interaction but is too afraid. Instead she calls another observer so the person can hear what is happening. Her hands are shaking and she can barely operate her phone.
- An agent wearing a bandana with stars on it tells them they must leave now and not follow again. Because ICE vehicles are blocking their car, they have to back into the street to leave.
- **After** - Biestman calls Richfield Police Department to file a report. They refuse, citing "federal pre-emption."

## Witness Account: Beatriz Rudolph Leon
Beatriz Rudolph Leon, 25, of Eagan — who grew up attending Assumption Church — was observing with her husband Ethan from across 77th Street when she saw ICE agents surrounding Biestman and Lee's car in the Roosevelt Park parking lot. She walked to the sidewalk to photograph the encounter. After agents honked and yelled at her, she returned to her car. Three to four ICE vehicles then pulled up and four agents pointed what appeared to be AR-15s at her and her husband.

Agent quotes directed at Leon and her husband:
- "Hit me and you'll see what happens" (when their car was still in Drive)
- "We know exactly who you are. You are a citizen so you think we can't do anything to you. But we will detain and hold you."
- "Record this. Now get out your little phone and record this."
- "We get what you are trying to do, but stop following us. We will detain you for interfering with our investigation."

A Richfield police officer arrived. The agents immediately put their weapons away. As they left, one agent walked back to Leon's car and said: "See what happens when you call the police? Nothing. They can't do shit to us."

## Affected Individual Statement

### John Biestman
"I have never had a gun pointed at me in my life. I was both terrified and angry that my government would treat me and my wife this way."

On the agents: "They had clearly just been hired off the streets and demonstrated zero professionalism. They had the professional demeanor of criminals and thugs."

"As senior citizens, my wife and I are appalled at the actions of the ICE agents. We find it incredible that the agents intended to kidnap parishioners from their church on a December Sunday morning."

### Janet Lee
"I have never had a gun pointed at me in my life, let alone had multiple guns pointed at me by representatives of my own government."

On the trauma: "I am tormented by the memory of this encounter. In the few days since this occurred, I am hypersensitive to my surroundings and suspicious that I am being followed. Every time I see a black SUV, I have a flash of anxiety because I know that the ICE agents know who I am and know our license plate number."

"Despite this traumatic experience, I have continued to engage in constitutional observation activities. Even though I am frightened for my safety, I feel an obligation to protest, to bear witness to ICE's cruelty and to disseminate information about what I observe. I wish that I was braver but I have faith that my courage will grow to meet this dire occasion."

### Beatriz Rudolph Leon
"I have never had an assault, or any, weapon shoved in my face. I felt as though if I said anything wrong, I would be killed."

## Legal Action

### Tincher v. Noem (Case 0:25-cv-04669-KMM-DTS)
Biestman and Lee are named plaintiffs in ACLU of Minnesota lawsuit filed December 17, 2025, challenging ICE's treatment of observers and protesters. The suit seeks a preliminary injunction limiting agents' use of firearms, chemical irritants, and arrests of citizens not resisting dispersal orders.

### January 13 Hearing
U.S. District Judge Katherine Menendez heard arguments on the preliminary injunction. DOJ attorney Jeremy Newman argued drivers were "chasing" ICE vehicles to draw crowds. Menendez pressed him, saying she had seen no evidence drivers were breaking traffic laws. Plaintiffs' attorney Kyle Wislocky argued following ICE vehicles is protected First Amendment activity. No ruling was issued at the hearing.

## Context
The incident occurred at a Catholic church as Sunday morning services were ending — one of the sensitive locations where faith leaders across Minneapolis-St. Paul organized to protect Somali communities from ICE operations. Both Biestman and Leon stated they intended to continue observing despite the threats.
