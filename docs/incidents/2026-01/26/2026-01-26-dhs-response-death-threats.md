# DHS Response: ICE Officers Face "8,000% Increase in Death Threats"; Publishes Minnesota Voicemail

## Summary
On January 26, 2026, DHS issued a press release claiming an 8,000% increase in death threats against ICE officers and their families. The release included the transcript of a threatening voicemail left for a Minnesota-based ICE officer on January 24, 2026. DHS blamed "sanctuary politicians" — naming Governor Tim Walz and Mayor Jacob Frey — for inciting the threats through their "repeated vilification and demonization of law enforcement." DHS has not provided underlying data supporting the 8,000% figure; this statistic was first used in an October 2025 press release.

## The Statement

DHS Assistant Secretary Tricia McLaughlin stated:

> "Make no mistake, threatening rhetoric and this unprecedented violence against our law enforcement is incited by sanctuary politicians through their repeated vilification and demonization of law enforcement."

### Voicemail Transcript (January 24, 2026)
The voicemail left for a Minnesota ICE officer included:

> "I hope your wife dies. I hope your mom and dad die...I hope everything wrong that could go in your life happens...I hope you get hit by a bus. I hope you're paralyzed, and your wife leaves you...You are a traitor to the American people...You should kill yourself. You're f---ing disgusting."

### Claims Made
- 8,000% increase in death threats against ICE officers and their families
- 1,300% increase in assaults (reiterated from [January 8 press release](#2026-01-08-dhs-response-radical-rhetoric))
- Officers face stalking, doxxing, and bounties on their heads
- Mexican drug cartels allegedly work with gangs to place bounties on ICE agents

## Fact-Check

The 8,000% death threat figure was first used by DHS in an October 30, 2025 press release — months before the January 26 release repackaged it with the Minnesota voicemail. DHS has not provided underlying data supporting this statistic. See [NPR fact-check](https://www.npr.org/2025/10/10/nx-s1-5565146/white-house-claims-more-than-1-000-rise-in-assaults-on-ice-agents-data-says-otherwise) and [CPR News investigation](https://www.cpr.org/2025/10/02/ice-agent-assault-claims-data-lacking/) of DHS assault statistics methodology.
