# Food Shelf Volunteer Detained at Basic Needs in Cottage Grove

## Summary

A middle-aged Latino pastor who volunteered at Basic Needs Food Market in Cottage Grove was detained by ICE agents on January 15, 2026. The man had been volunteering several times a week since October, providing translation services for Spanish-speaking customers. ICE agents surveilled the food pantry for two days before making the arrest in the parking lot.

## What Happened

ICE agents first appeared at Basic Needs Food Market on Monday, January 13, returned on Tuesday, and made the arrest on Wednesday, January 15.

According to Executive Director Opey Peñaloga, the volunteer took a break around 1 p.m. "As he was coming back in the doors, they just pulled up out of nowhere." Agents asked for documentation, and "whatever it was that he did show them wasn't enough to satisfy them. They arrested him on the spot, just at the doors of our food market."

The volunteer was placed in a black SUV. As of the time of news coverage, the organization had been unable to determine his location or status: "We don't know where he is. We don't know how he's doing."

## Community Response

**Washington County Board Chair Karla Bigham:** "This is completely unacceptable and egregious. These actions are not making our community safer."

**Cottage Grove Mayor Myron Bailey:** Expressed being "troubled" by the arrest and affirmed support for Basic Needs' mission.

## Impact on Food Pantry

Basic Needs reported:
- Noticeable decrease in shoppers of color due to fear
- Doubled food orders to address heightened community need
- Increased expenses for culturally specific food, especially for East African neighbors

The executive director stated: "We're continuing to ground our work in the belief that every person deserves access to basic needs and to be treated with humanity regardless of their circumstances."
