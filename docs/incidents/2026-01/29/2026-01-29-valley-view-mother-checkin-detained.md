# Asylum-Seeking Mother Detained at Check-In; Principal Brings Her Children to Whipple

## Updates
- **Feb 4** - Both children [released from ICE custody and returned to Minnesota](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/02/04/minnesota-boys-from-liam-ramos-school-released-from-ice-custody).

## Summary

An asylum-seeking mother of two Valley View Elementary students was detained during an immigration check-in on January 29, 2026. With no family in Minnesota to care for her children (ages 7-9), she asked Principal Jason Kuhlman to bring them to the Whipple Federal Building at Fort Snelling. The eldest child broke into tears upon entering the building.

## Affected Individual(s)

- **Mother:** Name not disclosed; asylum-seeker
- **Children:** Two students at Valley View Elementary, ages 7-9

## What Happened

The mother was detained Thursday morning during an immigration check-in. Principal Kuhlman confirmed she is currently seeking asylum.

With no one in Minnesota left to care for them, she asked Kuhlman to bring her two children to be with her at the Whipple Federal Building. The children are between 7 and 9 years old.

According to the Star Tribune, the eldest child "was stoic as Kuhlman described his mother's detainment" but "broke into tears and gripped the school nurse when walking into the building."

**Principal Kuhlman:** "We shouldn't have to be bringing students to a detention center because their mom was doing everything right and was detained."

He added: "We don't know if they will be taken to Texas. We're praying that's not the case."

## Context: Columbia Heights Schools Impact

Kuhlman said ICE has detained mothers and fathers from 25 families at Valley View Elementary alone, including 5-year-old Liam Conejo Ramos and his father on January 20.

Columbia Academy Principal Leslee Sherk says at least 14 families at her school have been affected by immigration enforcement. She now dreads Mondays: "Each Monday comes with voicemails detailing parents detained by ICE."

### Conditions at Detention Centers

Kuhlman expressed concern about conditions: "There's a lot of people getting sick. We have a fourth grader from Highland Elementary down there [in Dilley, Texas] and then we have Liam ... and they're both sick. The conditions aren't good. It's too crowded; they're not getting fed well. That's my fear."

A congressional visit revealed Liam Conejo Ramos showed signs of lethargy and depression.
