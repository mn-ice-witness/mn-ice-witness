# Ten-Year-Old Elizabeth Caisaguano Detained with Mother, Held Nearly One Month in Texas

## Updates
- **Feb 4** - Elizabeth and her mother [released from Dilley](https://sahanjournal.com/education/columbia-heights-fourth-grader-held-by-ice/) Tuesday evening after nearly one month in detention. They were at a Texas shelter Wednesday morning before returning to Minnesota to reunite with her father.
- **Feb 3** - [Habeas corpus petition filed](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/02/03/columbia-heights-girl-11-remains-in-ice-detention-in-texas-during-measles-outbreak). Judge Fred Biery issued order blocking removal or transfer, giving the government five days to respond.
- **Feb 3** - Elizabeth remains in ICE detention during [measles outbreak at Dilley facility](https://www.startribune.com/walz-demands-dhs-stop-detaining-minnesota-children/601575923). Gov. Walz demands DHS stop detaining Minnesota children.

## Summary
Elizabeth Zuna Caisaguano, a 10-year-old fourth grader at Highland Elementary in Columbia Heights, was detained by ICE agents with her mother on January 6, 2026, while on their way to school. During the arrest, Elizabeth called her father to say agents would drop her at school—but neither she nor her mother arrived. By day's end, both had been flown to the Dilley Immigration Processing Center in Texas. Elizabeth spent nearly one month in detention before being released on February 3, making her the first and longest-detained of the Columbia Heights students taken during the Trump administration's immigration crackdown.

## Timeline
- **January 6, 2026 (morning)** - Elizabeth and her mother detained by ICE agents on the way to school.
- **During arrest** - Elizabeth called her father. She acted as interpreter, telling him agents said they would drop her at school.
- **Later that morning** - Father Luis Zuna rushed to Highland Elementary and waited for hours with school staff. Elizabeth never arrived.
- **By end of day** - Elizabeth and her mother had been flown to the Dilley Immigration Processing Center in Dilley, Texas.

## Witness Accounts

### School Social Worker Tracy Xiong
Speaking at a press conference with Gov. Tim Walz on February 3:

> "That image of Elizabeth's father will stay with me forever. I watched him sit in his car, bury his head in his hands and cry uncontrollably."

### Principal Secretary Carolina Gutierrez (Highland Elementary)
On Elizabeth's detention:

> "When she was flown to Texas, she thought her dreams were over. She dreams of being a doctor. She's pleaded with her dad: 'Get me out of here. I want to go back home. I want to go back to school. I want to eat good food.' … Mom and Dad didn't understand why they were being treated like this. They were like, we didn't do anything wrong."

On the father's state:

> "He has been sad and desperate for answers. He hasn't been able to sleep. He doesn't have an appetite. He felt very helpless."

### Attorney Bobby Painter (Texas Immigration Law Council)
> "This didn't have to happen. This is a family going through the process as it was intended. They presented at the border as asylum seekers and were admitted to the country. That case is still ongoing. They did everything they were supposed to do and still found themselves detained and separated."

On Elizabeth:
> "She's an avid reader and really wants to get back into that routine."

## Health Concerns
By the time of release, both Elizabeth and her mother were ill:
- Elizabeth had flu-like symptoms.
- Her mother had broken out in hives.
- Neither had received a medical assessment, medicine, or testing.
- Hundreds of children detained at Dilley during an active measles outbreak at the facility.

## DHS Response
DHS defended the detention, stating that after Elizabeth's mother was arrested, "officers allowed her to make phone calls to place the child in the custody of someone she designated." DHS claimed: "She failed to find a trusted adult to care for the child, so officers kept the family together for the welfare of the child."

DHS also claimed Elizabeth's mother had a "final order of removal."

**Disputed by family and attorneys:** The father was ready to take custody. Attorney Bobby Painter disputed the "final order" claim, stating one judge had denied asylum but the family filed a timely appeal, which is still pending—meaning there is no final removal order.

## Context
Elizabeth was the first of at least four Columbia Heights students detained during the Trump administration's immigration enforcement surge in Minnesota. Her case was assigned to U.S. District Judge Fred Biery, who also ordered the release of 5-year-old Liam Conejo Ramos. Judge Biery described Liam's case as having "its genesis in the ill-conceived and incompetently implemented government pursuit of daily deportation quotas, apparently even if it requires traumatizing children."
