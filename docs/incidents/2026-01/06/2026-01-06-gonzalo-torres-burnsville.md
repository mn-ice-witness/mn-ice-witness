# Chilean Man Agrees to Voluntary Departure, Offers to Pay Own Airfare — Still Stuck in Louisiana Jail Five Weeks Later

## Summary
Gonzalo Torres, a Chilean national living in Burnsville, was arrested by ICE on January 6. He signed a voluntary departure agreement on January 8 and asked to buy his own plane ticket home, but was refused. Five weeks and three cancelled government flights later, he remains detained at Richwood Correctional Center in Louisiana, where he has described bathrooms overflowing with excrement and eating off the floor.

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Name:** Gonzalo Torres
- **Age:** 29
- **Nationality:** Chilean
- **Immigration status:** Overstayed visa
- **Occupation:** Bartender/waiter at a Peruvian restaurant in Burnsville; also worked for a landscaping company (grass cutting in summer, snow plowing in winter)
- **Criminal record:** Only Minnesota infraction was driving without a valid U.S. driver's license
- **Family:** Girlfriend Constanza Palma; they shared an apartment in Burnsville. Mother Erika Rios and younger sister were visiting Minnesota at the time of arrest.
- **Pre-arrest plans:** Torres and Palma were already planning to return to Chile in early February

## Timeline
- **Jan 6** - Arrested by ICE in Burnsville.
- **Jan 8** - Signed voluntary departure agreement. Asked to buy his own plane ticket to Chile; request denied.
- **Jan 15** - Shipped to El Paso, Texas. First government-organized flight cancelled.
- **Jan 25** - Transferred to Richwood Correctional Center in Monroe, Louisiana. Second flight cancelled.
- **Jan 28** - Third flight scheduled; also cancelled.
- **Jan 29** - Last contact with family. Family unable to reach him after this date.
- **Feb 6** - Still detained at Richwood with no clear timeline for departure. Chilean Consulate in Houston told the family there is only one deportation flight to Chile per month, with dozens of Chileans waiting; next flight expected around February 10.

## Detention Conditions
Torres told his family that bathrooms at Richwood were overflowing with excrement and that he had to eat off the floor. Running water and electricity were cut at times. ICE told Torres he did not have the right to an attorney or to go through a court process because he had overstayed his visa.

A USA Today / El Paso Times investigation published in October 2025 documented conditions at Richwood Correctional Center, where approximately 750 immigrant women were held. Detainees described crystallized jelly, rancid-smelling beans, and bologna with ice still frozen on it. The facility — operated by LaSalle Corrections — had backed-up toilets, bug infestations, mildewed showers, and allowed only one hour of outdoor sunlight per day. A GAO report recorded over 500 food service deficiencies across ICE detention facilities nationwide.
