# ICE Agent Calls Observer "Judith" by Name After Running Plates; Agents Then Drive to Couple's Home

## Summary
Judy and Noah Levy, a St. Paul couple, followed a caravan of eight ICE SUVs after receiving a tip that agents were staging in a nearby parking lot on January 6. After agents surrounded observers' cars and photographed license plates, a masked agent walked up to Judy's window and said "Hello Judith. How are you today?" — using her legal name, which she says no one uses unless they've run her plates. When the couple continued observing, the ICE caravan drove directly to the Levys' residential street.

## Timeline
- Judy received a message from a book club member that ICE agents appeared to be staging in a nearby parking lot.
- She and Noah drove over and were the first observers on scene.
- Eight SUVs filled with masked, armed agents left the parking lot; the Levys followed.
- After failing to shake observers, agents halted the caravan in the middle of traffic, surrounded observers' cars, and threatened arrest for impeding law enforcement.
- Agents photographed observers' license plates.
- A masked agent walked up to Judy's passenger side window: "Hello Judith. How are you today?"
- The couple continued following when the caravan started moving again.
- ICE vehicles turned onto the Levys' residential street.

## Witness Accounts

Judy Levy: "I wasn't raised to sit back and not do something when people are being kidnapped, and families are being terrorized, and good, hard-working people who make up the fabric of this community are being persecuted."

### Legal Response
ACLU attorney John Boehler said license plate readers and personal data in car registrations are only supposed to be accessed for active criminal investigations. "Following or observing or reporting on federal agencies or federal activities is not a criminal activity — it's protected First Amendment activity. To be using those cameras, to use those license plate readers, to surveil protesters has a chilling effect on First Amendment rights, and that's what we think the goal is."

Minnesota Driver and Vehicle Services said they do not provide ICE access to citizens' private data. ICE and DHS did not respond to requests for information about the tools they used to access Minnesotans' personal data.
