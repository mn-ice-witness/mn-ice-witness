# Ecuadorian Roofer, Wife, and 14-Year-Old Son Self-Deport After Five Years in Minnesota; Leave Three Adult Children Behind

## Summary
Segundo, 43, a roofer, his wife Maria, 40, a wreath factory worker, and their youngest son Jairo, 14, flew from Minneapolis to Quito, Ecuador on January 23, 2026 — the same day as the largest protest in Minnesota history. The family had lived in the U.S. since 2021, had no criminal record, and had an up-to-date immigration case with pending U visa applications. They left three adult children and a U.S. citizen grandson behind in Minnesota. Their daughter Natalie and her husband had already been detained at work in mid-January; Natalie and her baby were sent to a Texas hotel before being returned to Ecuador. The Star Tribune documented the family's final night and departure. They left their south Minneapolis apartment at dawn in -22 degree weather, afraid of being stopped on the way to the airport.

## What Happened

The family had been increasingly afraid to leave their south Minneapolis apartment as Operation Metro Surge raged around them. They stopped going to work and school, relied on donated food from friends and church members, and kept watching the street.

> "We didn't come here with bad intentions. We came here to work. Now we are afraid to stay. We're afraid to go outside."

Their daughter Natalie and her husband were detained at work in mid-January. Natalie and her baby Ethan were sent to a Texas hotel before agreeing to return to Ecuador. For weeks, Natalie did not know where her husband was being held.

In late December, the family decided Segundo, Maria, and Jairo would leave. Three adult children and a U.S. citizen grandson would stay behind.

> "We feel like prisoners. We don't make a lot of money in Ecuador, but at least we can enjoy life."

The night before their departure, family and friends gathered to say goodbye. A local bishop gave the family a blessing. It was -22 degrees when they arrived at MSP before dawn on January 23 — the same morning more than 100 ICE protesters would be arrested at the airport and an estimated 50,000-100,000 would march through downtown Minneapolis.

The family was not aware of the government's CBP Home app or Project Homecoming departure incentives. Even if they had been, they would not have applied — they were leery of the government and feared for family members staying behind.
