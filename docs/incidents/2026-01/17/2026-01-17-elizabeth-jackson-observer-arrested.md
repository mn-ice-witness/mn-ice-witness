# Observer Arrested by ICE After Being Threatened at Staging Area Ten Days Earlier

## Summary
Elizabeth ("Beth") Jackson, a Ramsey County resident and trained constitutional observer, was arrested by ICE on January 17, 2026, and taken to the Whipple Building. Ten days earlier, on January 7, Jackson had been documenting ICE agents at a staging area at 1440 University Avenue West in St. Paul when two agents approached her car and explicitly threatened her: one told her he knew her home address, had been to her home the previous night, and would be back that night. Jackson's arrest bore hallmarks of targeting — she was picked up by two men in gym shorts and sweatshirts in a white minivan, one of whom did not have ID identifying him as an agent. After her arrest, ICE showed up on her block in full tactical gear. She subsequently received a DHS letter under TSA letterhead alleging illegal conduct, threatening to revoke her TSA precheck status if she did not respond within 60 days — despite not having been charged with any crime. She now faces a felony allegation.

## Affected Individual(s)

### Elizabeth ("Beth") Jackson (Observer, Declarant)
- **Residence:** Ramsey County, MN
- **Role:** Trained constitutional observer (trained in October 2025)
- **Current legal status:** Facing felony allegation; TSA precheck status threatened

## Timeline

### January 6, 2026 — Prior Night
- According to an ICE agent's own statement to Jackson the following day, agents visited her home on this date.

### January 7, 2026 — ICE Staging Area, 1440 University Avenue West, St. Paul
- Jackson drives to a parking lot she has learned ICE is using as a staging area. Despite being scared, she goes to observe and document as a constitutional observer.
- She sees approximately 5-6 SUVs and 8-10 people in tactical gear. She shouts at agents to express opposition and honks her horn to signal ICE presence in the area.
- Two agents exit SUVs and approach her car. One is approximately 5'7" with a heavy build; the other approximately 5'11" with an average build. One has a "POLICE" patch on his back; the other wears a North Face brand coat. Both have firearms on their hips and wear gaiters covering their faces.
- **Threat:** One agent tells Jackson he knows her home address. He says he came to her home the previous night (January 6) and that he would be back tonight. His tone is condescending and deliberately frightening.
- As agents return to their SUVs, Jackson asks where they are going. An agent tells her they are going to her house and that he has her address.
- Jackson follows an ICE vehicle around St. Paul for 5-15 minutes until it speeds (45-50 mph in a 25 mph zone) and jumps a median, making it impossible to follow.

### January 17, 2026 — Arrest
- Jackson is picked up by ICE. The vehicle and officers appear unprepared or off-duty: two men in gym shorts and sweatshirts in a white minivan. One of the Border Patrol officers does not have any ID on him to show he is an agent.
- She is taken to the Whipple Building.

### After Arrest
- Days after being taken to Whipple, ICE shows up on Jackson's block in full tactical gear.
- Jackson receives a letter from DHS under a Transportation Security Administration letterhead alleging illegal conduct against a border patrol agent in Bloomington — even though she was picked up in St. Paul while observing agents. She interprets the mismatch and generic nature of the letter as evidence it is a form letter being sent to many observers.
- The letter states she has 60 days to defend her case or her TSA precheck status will be revoked.
- Jackson has not been formally charged but faces a felony allegation that could hang over her for five years.

## Affected Individual Statements

### Elizabeth Jackson

On the arrest: "Two men in gym shorts and sweatshirts in a white minivan that did not look like law enforcement. As a matter of fact, one of the border patrol officers didn't have any ID on him to show me that he was indeed an agent."

On the DHS letter: "That tells me that this is probably a form letter, and that they're sending out a lot of them."

On the TSA threat: "Mind you, I haven't been charged, so I don't even know what to defend myself against. How many ways can they inflict pain on me, personally and us collectively?"

On the pattern of retaliation: "Part of the punishment is the process, because now also I have a felony hanging over my head for five years."

On continuing to observe: "Don't be like me three months ago — and think that this is a fictitious possibility in some other city. I think we're a practice ground for something bigger, and people need to be ready. Get trained. Know your rights."

## Legal Action

### Tincher v. Noem (Case 0:25-cv-04669-KMM-DTS)
Jackson's declaration of the January 7 intimidation was filed January 12, 2026, as Document 63. The subsequent January 17 arrest — ten days after the threats — supports plaintiffs' claims that agents are retaliating against observers who document ICE activity.

### Pending Criminal Matter
Jackson faces a felony allegation but has not been formally charged. She also received a DHS/TSA letter threatening administrative consequences. The discrepancy between the letter alleging conduct in Bloomington and her actual arrest in St. Paul raises questions about the specificity and good faith of the allegations.

## Context
Jackson's case illustrates a pattern of escalating retaliation against observers. The sequence — agents visiting her home (Jan 6), explicitly threatening her with knowledge of her address (Jan 7), arresting her (Jan 17), showing up on her block in tactical gear (after arrest), and sending a DHS letter threatening TSA status (after arrest) — represents multiple layers of pressure applied to a single observer. MPR reported that approximately 30,000 people in Minnesota have been trained as observers. Jackson's experience demonstrates the personal risk that role now carries.
