# Indigenous Ecuadorian Mother Detained, Leaving 10 Children Including 5-Month-Old Infant

## Summary
Melida Rita Wampash Tuntuam, a 41-year-old Indigenous Ecuadorian mother who worked as an office cleaner, was detained by ICE in early January. She left behind 10 children, including a 5-month-old infant who is a U.S. citizen. Her 20-year-old son quit his restaurant job to care for his siblings. ICE agents surrounded the family's home twice seeking additional family members. Community volunteers have been sheltering the children at a safe house in south Minneapolis.

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Name:** Melida Rita Wampash Tuntuam
- **Age:** 41
- **Origin:** Ecuador (Indigenous)
- **Occupation:** Office cleaner
- **Citizenship:** Undocumented (entered U.S. in 2022 via Texas border)
- **Children:** 10 total:
  - 22-year-old daughter (has final removal order, along with three other family members)
  - 20-year-old son (quit restaurant job to care for siblings)
  - Six children under 16 years old
  - 5-month-old infant (U.S. citizen)
  - Two youngest children are U.S. citizens

## What Happened
Federal immigration agents arrived at the family's Minneapolis home, initially claiming they were sending a social worker to check on the younger children. Instead, armed masked immigration officers surrounded the house. ICE surrounded the home twice seeking additional family members.

The older children, with help from church volunteer Feliza Martinez and other community members, relocated their younger siblings to a safe house in south Minneapolis.

## Impact on Children
- 20-year-old son quit his restaurant job to care for his siblings full-time
- 5-month-old infant is struggling with the transition from breastfeeding
- Children are being sheltered at a community safe house
- Community volunteers through Source MN are delivering meals, supplies, and helping arrange emergency custody
