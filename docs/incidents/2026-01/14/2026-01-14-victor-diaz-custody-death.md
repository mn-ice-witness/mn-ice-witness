# Victor Manuel Díaz Dies in ICE Custody at Camp East Montana After Coon Rapids Arrest

## Summary
Victor Manuel Díaz, a 36-year-old Nicaraguan man living in Coon Rapids, died on January 14 at Camp East Montana in El Paso, Texas — eight days after ICE detained him at his workplace. ICE classified his death as a "presumed suicide," but his family and attorney dispute that conclusion. His autopsy was routed to a military hospital rather than the county medical examiner who had ruled the previous detainee death at the same facility a homicide. Díaz is the second of three Minnesotans to die since Operation Metro Surge began.

## Affected Individual(s)
- **Name:** Victor Manuel Díaz
- **Age:** 36
- **Nationality:** Nicaraguan
- **Citizenship:** Undocumented (entered U.S. March 2024)
- **Occupation:** Restaurant worker at a Korean restaurant in Coon Rapids; previously worked on a dairy farm
- **Family:** Survived by two sons (ages 10 and 15), mother María del Rosario Díaz García (65), and five siblings in Nicaragua
- **Background:** Farmer in Nicaragua who came to the U.S. in March 2024. Hospitalized for tuberculosis in Minneapolis in November 2025 for one month. Had worked at the Coon Rapids restaurant only nine days before his arrest.

## Timeline
- **Jan 6, 8:30 AM** - Díaz speaks with his mother in Nicaragua before leaving for work. She recalls his last words: "Mom, you take care of yourself too. May God take care of you too."
- **Jan 6, morning** - ICE agents arrive at the Korean restaurant in Coon Rapids and detain Díaz and three Indonesian coworkers. No warrant is presented. The restaurant is forced to operate at limited capacity.
- **Jan 6 – Jan 14** - Díaz is transferred to Camp East Montana, the nation's largest migrant detention center, at Fort Bliss in El Paso, Texas.
- **Jan 14** - Contract security staff find Díaz "unconscious and unresponsive in his room." A 911 call states staff found him "with his pants tied up to his neck." Paramedics find him without a heartbeat. He is pronounced dead.
- **Jan 15** - Family in Nicaragua is notified of his death.

## Autopsy Controversy
Díaz's autopsy was performed at William Beaumont Army Medical Center at Fort Bliss — not by the El Paso County Medical Examiner, who had handled the two previous deaths at the facility. The Army hospital does not release autopsy reports to the public, only to family members and investigators. Findings could take months.

Attorney Randall Kallinen had to search for where Díaz's body had been sent, discovering it had been transferred to the military hospital without family notification. "Why did they go against their previous practice?" Kallinen said.

This change came after the El Paso County Medical Examiner ruled the previous Camp East Montana death — that of Geraldo Lunas Campos on January 3 — a **homicide** caused by "asphyxia due to neck and torso compression," contradicting ICE's claim that Lunas Campos died during a suicide attempt.

Angélica César, a fellow at Human Rights Watch, said: "The more credible approach here would have been for local, independent authorities with no institutional ties to DHS to conduct the autopsy and the investigation."

ICE did not respond to questions about why the autopsy was sent to a military hospital.

## Family Statements

Díaz's brother, Jairo Lenín Díaz:
> "I think it's very odd that every time somebody in ICE custody dies, they say that that person killed themselves. In a place where they take everything from you, and they're watching you at all times, how in the world could that happen?"

> "My brother was a believer in the Lord, and so he knew that only God has power over our own lives. And if you take your own life, then you know that you're not going to go to a good place."

> "He was a person with a lot of strength who wanted to work hard, and who wanted to help his children."

His mother, María del Rosario Díaz García:
> "I don't believe for a minute."

> "The pain is very great. Whenever someone who is close to you dies, you feel a lot of pain. But especially in this case, it was my son and this is a child who I held in my arms and protected."

His brother, Yorlan Díaz:
> "He was not a criminal; he was looking for a better life and he wanted to help our mother."

## Official Accounts

### ICE Statement
ICE stated that "contract security staff found Díaz unconscious and unresponsive in his room" and classified the death as "a presumed suicide" pending investigation.

## Context: Camp East Montana Deaths
Díaz is the third detainee to die at Camp East Montana:

- **Geraldo Lunas Campos** (Jan 3, 2026): 55-year-old Cuban national. ICE initially said he died after "experiencing medical distress," later told AP he died during a "suicide attempt." The El Paso County Medical Examiner ruled his death a **homicide** caused by "asphyxia due to neck and torso compression." Witnesses described guards using a chokehold.
- **Francisco Gaspar-Andres** (Dec 3, 2025): 48-year-old Guatemalan who died of suspected liver and kidney failure.

Díaz is the fifth person to die in ICE custody in 2026.
