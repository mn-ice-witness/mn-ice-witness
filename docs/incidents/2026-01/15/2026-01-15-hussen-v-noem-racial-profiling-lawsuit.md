# ACLU Files Class Action Challenging Racial Profiling During Operation Metro Surge

## Updates
- **Feb 17-18** - Judge Tostrud held [two days of evidentiary hearings](https://www.fox9.com/news/immigrants-arrested-during-operation-metro-surge-testify-against-ice-practices-feb-2026). Six plaintiffs testified, including lead plaintiff Mubashir Hussen who described being [slammed against a stairwell doorframe](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/02/17/court-hearings-lawsuit-alleging-ice-agents-racially-profiled-us-citizens-minnesota); video of his arrest played in court. Judge [took the case under advisement](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/02/18/judge-hears-arguments-in-lawsuit-alleging-ice-racially-profiling-somali-and-latino-residents) for a preliminary injunction ruling.

## Summary
On January 15, 2026, the ACLU filed Hussen v. Noem — a class-action lawsuit on behalf of three U.S. citizens and a proposed class of people whose constitutional rights were violated by federal agents during Operation Metro Surge. The complaint alleges agents conducted suspicionless stops, warrantless arrests, and racial profiling targeting Somali and Latino communities. With more than 40 sworn declarations and amicus briefs from ten bar associations and the City of Minneapolis, the case has produced the most comprehensive evidentiary record of racial profiling during the operation. A two-day evidentiary hearing was held February 17-18, 2026; the preliminary injunction is under advisement.

## The Three Named Plaintiffs

All three were U.S. citizens detained based on their perceived race.

### Mubashir Khalif Hussen (Lead Plaintiff)
20-year-old Somali American. On December 9, 2025, tackled, choked, and detained for hours while walking to lunch in Cedar-Riverside. Agents refused to check his passport. On January 6, pepper-sprayed from a passing vehicle while recording from a public sidewalk.
See: [U.S. Citizen Tackled, Detained by ICE During Lunch Break](/entry/2025-12-09-mubashir-wrongful-detention)

### Mahamed Rufai Eydarus
25-year-old Somali American. On December 10, 2025, detained with his mother while shoveling snow. Agents questioned why they were speaking a "foreign language" and ordered his mother to remove her niqab. Livestreamers accompanying agents called them "illegal aliens."
See: [U.S. Citizen Questioned for Speaking Somali While Shoveling Snow](/entry/2025-12-10-mahamed-eydarus-citizen-check)

### Javier Doe
22-year-old Hispanic man born in Minnesota. In January 2026, Border Patrol agents tackled him after he declined to answer citizenship questions. They handcuffed him, pressed a knee into his neck, drove him around while playing loud music, then released him in a parking lot. He sought emergency medical care.
See: [Two U.S. Citizen Target Employees Detained and Injured](/entry/2026-01-08-target-richfield-employees)

## Legal Claims
1. **Fourth Amendment** — Suspicionless stops and arrests without warrants or probable cause
2. **Fifth Amendment Equal Protection** — Targeting people based on perceived Somali or Latino ethnicity
3. **Federal immigration law** — Officers may conduct warrantless arrests only with probable cause; agents relied on appearance alone

## Class Definitions
- All people stopped for immigration purposes in Minnesota since December 1, 2025
- Subclass of those subjected to warrantless arrests
- Subclass of those targeted based on race or ethnicity

## Timeline of the Litigation

### January 15, 2026 — Complaint Filed
Class-action complaint filed on behalf of three named plaintiffs. DHS called the allegations "disgusting, reckless, and categorically FALSE."

### January 16, 2026 — Preliminary Injunction Motion; Judge Recuses
Judge Magnuson recused; case reassigned to Judge Tostrud. Plaintiffs moved for preliminary injunction with 15 sworn declarations.

### January 21, 2026 — Scheduling Conference
Preliminary injunction hearing set for February 9.

### January 30, 2026 — Government Files Opposition
Government opposed with four declarations from federal officials (Bottjen, Harvick, Berry, MacKenzie), arguing agents use "reasonable suspicion" and citing Justice Kavanaugh's 2025 concurrence suggesting officers may consider appearance, language, or location.

### February 2, 2026 — Plaintiffs Supplement with 27+ New Declarations
Motion to supplement the record with additional declarations documenting incidents through late January 2026, including accounts of guns pointed, violent arrests, cars rammed, and agents saying "I don't care if you're a citizen."

### February 3, 2026 — Ten Bar Associations File Amicus
Minnesota State Bar Association, Hennepin County Bar Association, Ramsey County Bar Association, Minnesota Hispanic Bar Association, Somali American Bar Association, Minnesota Asian Pacific American Bar Association, Minnesota Association of Black Lawyers, Minnesota Lavender Bar Association, Minnesota Disability Bar Association, and Minnesota Women Lawyers filed jointly.

### February 4, 2026 — City of Minneapolis Files Amicus; Evidentiary Hearing Ordered
Judge Tostrud granted both amicus motions and ordered an evidentiary hearing.

### February 5, 2026 — Hearing Rescheduled
Evidentiary hearing moved to February 17, 2026 at 1:00 PM.

### February 17, 2026 — Day 1: Plaintiff Testimony
Six plaintiffs testified, all U.S. citizens. Video of Mubashir Hussen's Cedar-Riverside arrest was played in the courtroom. Hussen described being slammed against a stairwell doorframe, then against metal bars. Mahamed Eydarus testified the encounter changed his life: "I've made it strictly going to and from work, anything else, I just stopped doing." Ramon Menera Romero testified he was handcuffed after agents heard his accent while he recorded from his own yard.

### February 18, 2026 — Day 2: Legal Arguments
Judge Tostrud heard oral arguments on the preliminary injunction. He questioned ICE's expansive interpretation of "likely to escape" warrantless arrest authority. The government argued operations "adhere to constitutional policies" with "no evidence of racial or ethnic profiling." ACLU attorney Kate Huddleston pointed to patterns across the sworn declarations. Judge took the case under advisement.
