# MSU Mankato Student Mohammed Hoque Detained by ICE a Second Time

## Summary
Mohammed Hoque, a 20-year-old Bangladeshi student at Minnesota State University, Mankato, was detained by ICE for a second time in mid-January 2026 during Operation Metro Surge. He was released on January 15 after several hours in custody. Hoque had previously been detained for 40 days in 2025 over pro-Palestinian social media posts, prompting a federal lawsuit. A federal judge found credible evidence of viewpoint-based targeting and ordered his release in May 2025.

## January 2026 Detention
During Operation Metro Surge, Hoque was detained by ICE for a second time in mid-January 2026. He was released on January 15 after several hours in custody. The release was announced during a protest of over 100 students at MSU Mankato's Centennial Student Union Mall, organized by the student group Mavericks for Change.

Cole Koets, president of Mavericks for Change, was calling for Hoque's release when a member of the crowd informed him Hoque had just been released. Koets told the crowd: "It was fantastic. ... It filled me with that same feeling of renewed hope that we can see justice in our communities for what's been happening lately."

## Background: March–May 2025 Detention
Hoque was first detained on March 28, 2025, when plainclothes immigration officers followed him home from a coding class, surrounded him at his residence, and told him his visa had been revoked. DHS terminated his SEVIS record while he was being processed at Fort Snelling. He was held at Freeborn County Jail in Albert Lea for 40 days.

His federal lawsuit alleged he was targeted for pro-Palestinian social media posts, including sharing posts about violence against Palestinians in Gaza and including "#FreePalestine" in his Instagram profile. DHS cited a 2023 disorderly conduct charge (pushing his brother's friend after a disagreement) as evidence of being a "threat to U.S. public safety," though this offense is not considered removable under immigration law.

On May 5, 2025, U.S. District Court Judge Jerry Blackwell issued a 17-page ruling ordering Hoque's release, finding "sufficiently clear evidence of viewpoint-based targeting for Petitioner's exercise of protected speech on a matter of public concern." Hoque was released May 6 on $7,500 bond.
