# Ecuadorian Teen Labeled "Unaccompanied Minor" by ICE, Sent to Michigan Shelter; Family Searched for Him for a Week

## Summary
A 16-year-old Ecuadorian asylum-seeker was detained by ICE while driving alone in north Minneapolis in early January. Despite having a father living in the U.S., agents labeled him an "unaccompanied minor," assigned him a new alien number, and transferred him to a federally contracted youth shelter in Michigan. The government lost track of his whereabouts for nearly a week while his family and attorney searched for him. His attorney traced his location through phone records and retrieved him in person on January 26. (Exact date of detention unknown; estimated as January 5 based on timeline details in reporting.)

## Timeline
- **Early January** - Sebastian detained by ICE while driving in north Minneapolis. He called his father before agents confiscated his phone.
- **Same day** - Taken to Bloomington, moved between holding areas, then held overnight in a hotel guarded by four agents.
- **Next day** - Flown to Michigan and placed at Bridgeway, a youth shelter run by Bethany Christian Services in Grand Rapids.
- **During detention** - Allowed supervised phone calls to father but told not to disclose location. Given a new alien number, making him virtually untraceable.
- **After federal judge ordered government response** - ICE attorney Julie Le wrote that "it does not appear that this Petitioner is in ICE custody."
- **Jan 26** - Attorney Claire Glenn traced phone calls to Bridgeway and arrived in person to claim him. Released in exchange for dismissing the wrongful-detainment case.

## Official Accounts

### DHS/ICE Statement
DHS did not respond to questions about Sebastian's case. DHS has denied that ICE targets children or teenagers but stated that if ICE "encounters" a minor without their parent, agents "ensure their safety" by working with other arms of the federal government.
