# Lakeville Business Owner Deported to Mexico Despite Two Court Orders

## Summary

Emilio Pena Jimenez, a tile installation business owner who has lived in the U.S. since 2000 with no criminal record, was arrested without a warrant at a Walmart in Apple Valley on January 24. Despite two federal court orders — a January 30 temporary restraining order and a February 2 order demanding his immediate release in Minnesota — ICE transported him to the El Paso-Juarez border and released him into Mexico. His attorney alleges he was denied diabetes medication and attorney access in detention, and signed voluntary departure documents under duress.

## Timeline

- **Jan 24** - Arrested without a warrant at Apple Valley Walmart; detained at Fort Snelling
- **Jan 30** - U.S. District Judge Jeffrey Bryan issued a temporary restraining order halting his transfer out of Minnesota and ordering his return by February 2
- **~Feb 2** - ICE transported Pena Jimenez to the El Paso-Juarez border and released him into Mexico
- **Feb 2** - Judge Bryan issued a second order demanding immediate release in Minnesota "without conditions"
- **Feb 5** - Attorney Joseph Kantor filed for contempt of court

## Detention Conditions

According to his attorney, Pena Jimenez was denied his regular diabetes medication and was not allowed to call an attorney despite several requests. His attorney alleges he was compelled to sign voluntary departure documents "he did not understand" while under duress from medication deprivation.

The government argues Pena Jimenez agreed to voluntarily leave the country two days before the court orders were issued.

## Court Order Defiance Pattern

This is at least the third documented case where federal authorities have defied Minnesota court orders during Operation Metro Surge. See also: [Tobay Robles Lyons](#2025-12-29-tobay-robles-lyons-deported) and the [Velasco family](#2026-01-22-velasco-family-hopkins).
