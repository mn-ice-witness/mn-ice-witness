# Minnesota Health Commissioner: Operation Metro Surge Causing Measurable Public Health Damage Statewide

## Summary
Dr. Brooke Cunningham, Commissioner of the Minnesota Department of Health, filed a sworn declaration on January 22, 2026, documenting the measurable impact of Operation Metro Surge on public health services across Minnesota. The declaration, filed in State of Minnesota v. Noem (Case 26-cv-190), details cascading failures across the state's public health infrastructure: a 40% increase in no-shows at federally qualified health centers, dramatic drops in HIV testing among Latino populations, cancellation of vaccine clinics and door-to-door outreach, families isolating to the point of storing garbage inside their homes rather than going outside, declining participation in WIC infant nutrition programs, a decrease in Spanish speakers calling for cancer and heart health screening, and a notable increase in emergency department visits for protest-related injuries since January 7. Non-white MDH staff conducting field work have reported being followed by federal law enforcement vehicles even while driving state-issued cars.

## Declarant

### Dr. Brooke Cunningham
- **Title:** Commissioner, Minnesota Department of Health (since 2023)
- **Prior roles:** Assistant Commissioner for the Health Equity Bureau; Assistant Professor, General Internist, and Sociologist in the Department of Family Medicine and Community Health at the University of Minnesota
- **Education:** Doctorates in medicine and sociology (University of Pennsylvania); residency in internal medicine (Duke University); fellowships in health services research, health policy, and bioethics (Johns Hopkins University)

## Documented Impacts

### Direct MDH Program Impacts

**Vaccine Preventable Disease / Immunization Program**
- Mobile units and town halls for vaccine confidence have been cancelled due to community fears about appearing in public spaces.

**Food, Pools, and Lodging Services**
- Food inspection staff have modified inspection schedules to avoid areas with the highest ICE-related safety concerns.
- Some food and beverage establishments have closed due to ICE-related safety concerns, requiring further schedule modifications.

**Environmental Surveillance & Assessment (Blood Lead Screening)**
- MDH staff have observed a decline in their ability to engage with families of children with elevated blood lead levels, particularly Hispanic and Somali families.
- Non-white MDH field staff have reported being followed or tailed by federal law enforcement vehicles, even when driving state-issued vehicles.

**Disease Intervention Services (STI/HIV)**
- Staff are limiting field work due to safety concerns.
- Staff who must go into the field are now going in pairs due to concerns about potential unlawful detentions, arrests, or profiling by DHS agents, straining MDH resources.

**Cancer and Heart Health Screening Programs**
- A significant decrease in Spanish speakers calling to schedule cancer or heart health screening appointments.

### MDH Partner Organization Impacts

**WIC (Women, Infants, and Children)**
- Local WIC agencies report a decrease in recipients attending appointments for infant health screening, lactation support, and help accessing healthy food and infant formula.

**HIV Prevention Services**
- Grantees providing HIV prevention to predominantly Latino populations report that testing numbers have dropped dramatically. Although staff continue community outreach, people are not showing up.

**Refugee Health Program**
- At least two out of three refugee resettlement agencies in Minnesota are declining new arrivals and redirecting them away from Minnesota due to safety concerns.

**Health Promotion and Chronic Disease Division**
- Community partners are cancelling in-person activities including vaccine clinics and door-to-door outreach.
- Partners report difficulty providing chronic disease education when people are focused on immediate safety and basic needs.

### Healthcare Provider Impacts

**Emergency Departments**
- As of January 16, MDH emergency department surveillance data indicated a notable increase since January 7, 2026, in ER visits for injuries potentially related to protest activity. Injuries identified through text fields (e.g., "protest," "rally," "tear gas," "pepper spray") and diagnostic codes (e.g., toxic effect of tear gas).

**Family Home Visiting Program**
- Local agencies report families isolating themselves to the point of delaying medical care — including prenatal and post-partum appointments — and storing garbage inside their homes instead of bringing it outside to driveways or alleyways.
- Home visitors report families worried about eviction due to inability to go to work.

**Federally Qualified Health Centers**
- A 40% increase in the rate of no-shows for appointments.
- Increased number of patients requesting virtual appointments, which limits the type of care and screenings that can be provided.

## Legal Action

### State of Minnesota v. Noem (Case 0:26-cv-00190-KMM-DJF)
Dr. Cunningham's declaration was filed January 22, 2026, as Document 76. This case was filed by the State of Minnesota (through Attorney General Keith Ellison) and the Cities of Minneapolis and St. Paul, seeking to block the federal government from "implementing the unprecedented surge in Minnesota." The lawsuit alleges that "thousands of armed and masked D.H.S. agents have stormed the Twin Cities to conduct militarized raids and carry out dangerous, illegal and unconstitutional stops and arrests."

## Context
The Cunningham declaration is notable for documenting the measurable, cascading public health consequences of a federal enforcement operation — not through individual stories but through institutional data. The impacts span the full range of public health services: infant nutrition, cancer screening, HIV prevention, vaccine delivery, food safety inspection, blood lead screening, prenatal care, and emergency medicine. The detail about non-white MDH field staff being followed by federal vehicles while in state-issued cars suggests that even state government employees conducting official health work are being surveilled or intimidated. The declaration was executed under penalty of perjury by one of the state's highest-ranking public health officials.
