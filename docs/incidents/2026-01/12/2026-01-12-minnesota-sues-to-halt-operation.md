# Minnesota Sues Federal Government to Halt Operation Metro Surge

## Summary
Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison, Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey, and St. Paul Mayor Kaohly Her filed a federal lawsuit against the Department of Homeland Security to halt Operation Metro Surge. The complaint called the operation a "federal invasion of the Twin Cities" and alleged systematic racial profiling, violations of the First and Tenth Amendments, and political retaliation against Minnesota for its voting patterns. It was the first time a state sued the federal government to halt an immigration enforcement operation. On January 31, U.S. District Judge Katherine Menendez denied the request for a preliminary injunction, allowing the operation to continue.

## Key Details
- The lawsuit was filed less than a week after ICE agent Jonathan Ross fatally shot Renee Good on January 7
- Defendants named included DHS Secretary Kristi Noem, acting ICE Director Todd Lyons, and CBP Commander Greg Bovino
- The complaint noted Minnesota's noncitizen immigrant population is only 1.5%, well below the national average, questioning why the state was targeted
- By January 9, Minneapolis Police had logged over 3,000 overtime hours (approximately $2 million) responding to the surge
