# Agents Smash Car Window, Pin Man Until Unconscious at Speedway; Bystander Filming Tackled and Detained

## Updates
- **2026-02-08**: Identified primary target as Orbin Mauricio Henriquez-Serrano per family GoFundMe and BringMeTheNews. Added details of his injuries (recent knee surgery, lost consciousness, neck trauma, stomach/facial/chest wounds), denial of medical treatment, transfer to Camp East Montana in El Paso, TX. Added sister's account, GoFundMe details, BringMeTheNews follow-up, and NYT coverage. Changed injuries to "serious."

## Summary
At a Speedway gas station in St. Paul, federal agents smashed Orbin Mauricio Henriquez-Serrano's car window, forcibly dragged him out, and pinned him to the ground with knees on his back and head until he lost consciousness. Henriquez-Serrano, who had recently undergone leg surgery, suffered neck trauma, and visible stomach, facial, and chest wounds. He was transferred to a detention facility in El Paso, Texas, where his sister reported he had not received any medical evaluation or treatment. A bystander filming the arrest, 22-year-old U.S. citizen Lalo Rios Meza, was told to back up, complied, but was then shoved, tackled, and detained for approximately 6-7 hours. Border Patrol Commander Gregory Bovino was personally present at the scene.

## Affected Individual(s)

### Orbin Mauricio Henriquez-Serrano (Primary Target)
- **Citizenship:** Honduran national (per DHS); born in Honduras
- **Status:** Had 2020 removal order (per DHS)
- **Criminal record:** Family states no criminal record in U.S. or Honduras; Minnesota court records search produced no results
- **Medical condition:** Had recently undergone surgery on his leg (knee)
- **Detained at:** Camp East Montana, El Paso, Texas

### Lalo Rios Meza (Bystander Arrested)
- **Age:** 22
- **Citizenship:** U.S. Citizen
- **What happened:** Filming, told to back up, complied, then shoved, tackled, detained. Rios Meza told Status Coup: "I was recording them talking to the guy in the jeep, they told me to step back, I complied and seconds later an agent rushed at me and swung at my face and got tackled."
- **Detained:** Approximately 6-7 hours at federal building at Fort Snelling
- **Status:** Released Sunday night

## Timeline
- **~Afternoon, January 11** — Henriquez-Serrano is at the Speedway gas station at Snelling and Portland avenues in St. Paul.
- **Agents approach** — Multiple federal agents surround his vehicle and demand he exit. He is given only seconds to comply.
- **Window smashed** — When Henriquez-Serrano does not immediately exit, agents smash his car window. Getty Images photographer Scott Olson captures the moment.
- **Forcibly removed** — Henriquez-Serrano is dragged from the car, taken to the ground, and physically restrained by multiple agents.
- **Pinned until unconscious** — Agents place knees on his back and head. He loses consciousness. Photos show boot prints on his body while unconscious. He is dragged into an unmarked vehicle.
- **Bystander filming** — Rios Meza is recording the arrest from nearby. Gregory Bovino, Border Patrol Commander, is on scene and yells at bystanders to back up.
- **Rios Meza tackled** — After being told to back up and complying, Rios Meza says "Don't touch me" when shoved. An agent rushes him, swings at his face, and he is tackled to the ground by multiple agents.
- **Rios Meza detained** — Taken to federal building at Fort Snelling, held approximately 6-7 hours, released Sunday night.
- **Henriquez-Serrano transferred** — Moved to Camp East Montana detention facility in El Paso, Texas.
- **January 13 (Tuesday)** — Henriquez-Serrano's sister briefly speaks to him. He reports he has not received any medical evaluation or medical attention despite significant pain, especially to his surgically repaired leg.

## Context
Henriquez-Serrano's family confirmed he is an undocumented immigrant but stated he has no criminal record in either the United States or Honduras. A search of Minnesota court records produced no results. The NYT reported that Bovino was personally on scene at the gas station directing operations. The level of force used — smashing a window, multiple agents pinning a man until unconscious, visible wounds — was captured on video from multiple angles and received national media coverage. The fact that Henriquez-Serrano had recently undergone leg surgery and was denied medical treatment while detained adds a medical neglect dimension to the incident. Photos circulating on social media show boot prints visible on his unconscious body as he was dragged to an unmarked vehicle.
