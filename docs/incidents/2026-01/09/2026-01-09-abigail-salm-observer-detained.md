# "Is This How You Want to Die?" — Observer Detained, Threatened with Bullet, Phone Stolen by Agent

## Summary
Abigail Salm, a 27-year-old U.S. citizen, pulled over to film after ICE agents brake-checked a convoy of observers near Knollwood Mall in Hopkins on January 9, 2026 — 48 hours after the killing of Renee Good. An agent in tactical gear told her "Have y'all not learned from the last couple of days?", then snatched her phone. When she asked what she would be arrested for, the agent said "Ok you're under arrest." While detained, an armed agent told her: "You don't look like you could be more than 18 years old. Is this how you want to die? With a bullet in your skull?" Agents told her they would let her go if she had "learned her lesson." When she responded defiantly, they reversed course. Because Salm has POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome), she fainted twice after being yanked to her feet — agents said "She's faking." An agent took her phone from the hood of her car and put it in his own pocket; when she demanded it back, he pointed at "a random brown guy" and said "That guy took it," then argued for 10-15 minutes before returning to her car and pretending to find it. Nearby observers were shot with rubber bullets during her detention. No one checked her ID or citizenship status.

## Timeline
- **Jan 9, ~morning** — Concerned citizens follow a convoy of unmarked ICE vehicles from the Knollwood Mall area in Hopkins.
- **Brake-check collision** — An ICE agent accelerates then slams on brakes, causing a minor collision with a Honda CR-V behind him.
- **Salm films** — Salm, trailing two cars back, pulls over to film as agents in camouflage pull two occupants from the SUV.
- **Agent confronts Salm** — Agent in tactical gear approaches: "Listen. Have y'all not learned from the last couple of days?"
- **Salm responds** — "Learned what? What's our lesson here? What do you want us to learn?"
- **Phone snatched** — Agent says "Following federal agents..." with an expletive, and snatches the phone from her hand.
- **Threatened with arrest** — "You need to leave now or you're going to be arrested." Salm asks what she would be arrested for. "Ok you're under arrest."
- **Death threat** — Armed agent tells her: "You don't look like you could be more than 18 years old. Is this how you want to die? With a bullet in your skull?"
- **On the ground** — Agents order her to the ground.
- **Conditional release** — "We're gonna let you go. But first: have you learned your lesson?"
- **Defiance** — Salm responds: "What, that you're domestic terrorists? Fuck you."
- **Release revoked** — "Oh, we were going to let you go. But not now."
- **Rubber bullets** — Agents begin shooting nearby observers with rubber bullets while Salm is on the ground.
- **Fainting (first)** — Agents yank Salm to her feet. Because she has POTS, she faints.
- **"She's faking"** — When she regains consciousness, an agent says: "See? She's faking."
- **Fainting (second)** — She faints again. An ambulance arrives. She is uncuffed.
- **Phone theft** — Upon regaining consciousness, Salm sees an agent grab her phone from the hood of her car and put it in his own pocket. She demands it back. The agent points at a nearby person of color: "That guy took it."
- **10-15 minute argument** — After extended argument, the agent walks back to her car and pretends to find the phone there.
- **Released** — Salm released at the scene. No one ever checked her ID or asked about her citizenship status.

Salm filed a sworn declaration (Document 69) in the ACLU of Minnesota's federal lawsuit challenging ICE treatment of observers. See: [ACLU Sues to Protect Observers](/entry/2025-12-17-tincher-v-noem-aclu-lawsuit)

## Context
The encounter occurred 48 hours after ICE agent Jonathan Ross [fatally shot Renee Good](/entry/2026-01-07-renee-good-shooting) in south Minneapolis. The agent's opening line — "Have y'all not learned from the last couple of days?" — explicitly invoked Good's killing as a warning to observers. The Intercept documented multiple agents using similar language in separate encounters during this period, including the [Phil Maddox encounter](/entry/2026-01-11-phil-maddox-did-you-not-learn) two days later. The death threat ("bullet in your skull"), the conditional release tied to "learning a lesson," and the theft of her phone demonstrate a pattern of intimidation targeting observers that the ACLU lawsuit seeks to address.
