# ICE Agents Attempt to Detain Indigenous Observer Near Powwow Grounds; Community Forces Retreat

## Summary
Rachel Dionne-Thunder, vice president of the Indigenous Protector Movement and a member of the Plains Cree (Big Stone Cree Nation), was sitting in her car near Powwow Grounds coffee shop on the American Indian Cultural Corridor while serving as an ICE observer on patrol. Federal agents surrounded her vehicle, demanded she lower her windows and unlock her door, and prepared to break her window with a window-breaker tool. Members of the Many Shields Society and community members converged on the scene, forcing agents to retreat. Dionne-Thunder recorded the encounter on Facebook Live.

## Timeline
- **Early morning, Jan 9** - Dionne-Thunder is stationed in her car near Powwow Grounds on Franklin Avenue, on observer patrol.
- **Minutes later** - ICE agents surround her vehicle. They demand she lower her windows, unlock her door, and provide identification.
- **Agents escalate** - Officers prepare a window-breaker tool to smash the vehicle window.
- **Community response** - Members of the Many Shields Society and other community members arrive at the scene.
- **Agents retreat** - Federal agents withdraw from the scene without making an arrest.

## Context
The confrontation occurred the day after ICE agent Jonathan Ross fatally shot Renee Good on January 8. The Many Shields Society and American Indian Movement's Grand Governing Council regularly patrol the Cultural Corridor, a tradition dating to AIM's founding in 1968. Following the incident, Minnesota tribal nations — including Leech Lake Band, Mille Lacs Band, White Earth Nation, and Bois Forte Band — began issuing identification cards to protect members.
