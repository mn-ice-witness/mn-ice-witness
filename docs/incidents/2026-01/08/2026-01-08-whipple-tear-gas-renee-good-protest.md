# Border Patrol Chief Bovino and Agents Tear Gas Peaceful Protesters at Whipple Building Morning After Renee Good's Killing

## Summary
On the morning of January 8, 2026 — less than 24 hours after ICE agent Jonathan Ross fatally shot Renee Good — hundreds of protesters gathered at the Bishop Henry Whipple Federal Building at Fort Snelling. Border Patrol Chief Greg Bovino was on-site. Without warning, agents launched two to three canisters of chemical agents into the crowd of peaceful protesters, followed by additional rounds as they pushed people away from the building. Theresa del Rosario, a 63-year-old massage therapist using a cane after recent knee surgery, was shoved hard between her shoulder blades by an agent standing near Bovino while she was trying to leave as instructed. When she asked the agent "Would you push your mother?" he replied, "Yeah, if she didn't listen to me." Her husband Thomas Ett, a 59-year-old computer engineer, was also affected by the chemical spray and repeatedly shoved in a crosswalk while trying to leave. AFL-CIO marshals in yellow vests had been organizing a nonviolent protest; after they left the immediate area, agents became more aggressive. Multiple arrests were made.

## Timeline
- **~6:45 AM** - Del Rosario and Ett arrive at the area outside Gate 1 of the Whipple Building. Protesters are already gathered. AFL-CIO marshals in yellow high-visibility vests are advising protesters to stay away from the Whipple property line, remain calm, and stay dedicated to nonviolent protest. Signs read "Don't take the bait."
- Del Rosario and Ett lead protesters in singing "Lead with Love." The crowd sings with them.
- **~7:00-7:30 AM** - More protesters arrive. Marshals keep people on the east side of the street across from the Whipple Building. Vehicles are entering the building area during this time.
- Marshals instruct protesters to move into the street. A vehicle attempting to enter Gate 1 is blocked by the crowd. Marshals keep protesters from crossing to the west side.
- Approximately a dozen agents are near the protesters, with about half a dozen directly in front of del Rosario. Agents are in tactical vests and utility belts, carrying chemical spray including what appear to be projectile launchers. All have faces covered except one: **Greg Bovino**, Border Patrol Chief, recognized by both del Rosario and Ett. He is the shortest man in the line of agents, wearing a helmet or hat with Border Patrol insignia. Agents have numbers marked on their vest chests in yellow tape. Many wear Border Patrol badges.
- Ett observes agents taking aggressive stances. One smaller male agent stands in a "Superman-like pose" with hands on hips, frequently putting his hand on his gun.
- **~7:45-8:00 AM — Chemical agents deployed** - Without warning, agents launch two to three canisters of chemical spray at the protesters. A cloud of smoke envelops the dozens of protesters. Del Rosario, approximately 100 feet from the launch point, is severely affected — she cannot stop coughing and struggles to breathe. Ett also experiences stinging eyes and difficulty breathing. Ett does not hear any warning before deployment.
- Marshals encourage protesters to let the vehicle pass through the gate. Protesters comply, then close the line.
- **More agents arrive** - The number of agents at Gate 1 grows to approximately two dozen. Del Rosario observes one male agent run toward a female protester and push her to the ground. Another male agent pulls a female protester onto Whipple Building property. Agents appear more aggressive toward female protesters. Ett observes only one protester throw a single snowball the entire morning.
- **~8:00-8:15 AM — Escalation** - Agents begin pushing protesters off the street. AFL-CIO marshals announce they are leaving and depart the Gate 1 area. After the marshals leave, agents become more aggressive. They launch more chemical spray and repeatedly yell "You need to leave." Del Rosario tells them she is in a public space with a legal right to be there.
- **Del Rosario shoved** - As del Rosario tries to leave through a crowd being funneled through a narrow space (40-50 people, room for only 2-3 at a time), a tall African American agent gently puts an arm on her and says, "Well, move at your pace." He is then replaced by Greg Bovino and a Latinx agent. When del Rosario again says "I'm moving at my own pace," the Latinx agent shoves her hard between the shoulder blades. She stumbles but is caught by the crush of people and her cane. She turns around and asks, "Would you push your mother?" The agent responds: **"Yeah, if she didn't listen to me."** She tries to make eye contact with Bovino, who avoids her gaze.
- **Ett shoved** - While trying to leave through a crosswalk with his wife, Ett is shoved repeatedly. An agent responds angrily to del Rosario saying she is moving at her own pace: "We've given you plenty of time," shoving her hard as she uses her cane. No cars are trying to come through. Protesters are not pushing back. They are in the crosswalk heading toward the sidewalk, trying to leave as instructed.
- Del Rosario observes people being detained: one person face down on the ground with hands behind their back, taken into the Whipple Building area; two others sitting on the ground inside Gate 1 with hands behind their backs.

## Witness Accounts

### Thomas Ett
On the protest atmosphere: "Other than seeing one protestor throw a single snowball, I did not see any of the dozens of protestors around us take any threatening action against agents."

On the chemical spray: "I did not hear any warning before it was sprayed. It seemed like it came in multiple rounds, and the repeated exposure made it worse, stinging my eyes and making it hard to breathe."

On the proportionality: "This overwhelming show of force from federal agents was crazy compared to other protests I have been to. I usually march and chant, the protest maybe blocks some cars for a while, and then I go on my way. Instead, here it was a very aggressive show of force that was disproportionate to the protestors' activity."

On the shove of his wife: "This was so unnecessary. Protesters weren't pushing back. There were no cars trying to come through. We were in the street in the crosswalk heading towards the sidewalk outside the Whipple Building area and trying to leave as instructed. There was no need for the agents to shove people or escalate. It seemed like the reason for the agents' aggression was that they were trying to assert their power and dominance."

### Theresa del Rosario
On the shove: "I felt a very hard push between my shoulder blades. The force made me stumble; were it not for the crush of people in front of me and my cane righting my feet, I would have fallen. I turned around, and to the Latinx agent, I asked, 'Would you push your mother?' He responded, 'Yeah, if she didn't listen to me.' I tried to make eye contact with Bovino, who avoided my gaze."

On the agents' behavior: "I noticed that the agents seemed to be avoiding making eye contact with me. I think avoiding eye contact is a way to avoid feeling your conscience."

On why she protests: "Even though it takes time and money away from my business, I feel the need to dedicate myself to protest and supporting my community during this time in our country's history."

## Injuries
- **Theresa del Rosario:** Chemical spray caused severe coughing and difficulty breathing despite being approximately 100 feet from launch point. Shoved hard between shoulder blades while using a cane, nearly fell. Voice still affected two days later. Knee sore and stiff from the incident.
- **Thomas Ett:** Chemical spray caused stinging eyes, difficulty breathing, coughing, and running sinuses for the rest of the day. Shoved repeatedly in crosswalk.

## Official Presence
- **Border Patrol Chief Greg Bovino** was on-site at the Whipple Building, identified independently by both del Rosario and Ett. WISN reported Bovino was "checking on federal agents and thanking them for the continued increased enforcement." Protesters shouted at him for the hour he was outside.
- Agents wore Border Patrol badges and had numbers marked in yellow tape on their vests.
- Minneapolis Public Schools canceled school, sports, and activities for Thursday and Friday due to safety concerns.
- Gov. Walz placed the National Guard on standby.

## Legal Action

### Tincher v. Noem (Case 0:25-cv-04669-KMM-DTS)
Ett's declaration (Doc. 62) and del Rosario's declaration (Doc. 61) were both filed January 12, 2026. Their accounts describe the deployment of chemical agents against a crowd engaged in nonviolent protest, with organized marshals maintaining discipline, and the use of force against a woman with a cane who was trying to leave as instructed.

## Context
This protest was organized within hours of Renee Good's killing on January 7. The presence of Border Patrol Chief Greg Bovino — the highest-ranking field official in Operation Metro Surge — at the scene where agents tear gassed peaceful protesters is significant. Del Rosario's account describes a clear escalation pattern: agents became more aggressive after the AFL-CIO marshals departed, suggesting they were emboldened by the loss of organized de-escalation. The contrast between the African American agent who told del Rosario to "move at your pace" and the Latinx agent who shoved her moments later illustrates the range of individual agent behavior within the same operation.
