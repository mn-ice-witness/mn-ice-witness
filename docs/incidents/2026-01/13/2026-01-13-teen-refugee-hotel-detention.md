# Teen Refugee Handcuffed Driving to School, Held Overnight in Hotel Room with Federal Agents

## Summary
A high school junior living in St. Cloud was pulled over by ICE while driving to school on January 13. Despite showing her driver's license proving she is a minor, agents handcuffed her in the street and forced her to abandon her father's car. At the Whipple Federal Building, an agent told her to call her family — then threatened that if they came to pick her up, ICE would send the entire family to Texas. The teen spent the night in a hotel room with two federal agents before her attorney retrieved her the next day. At no point did anyone interview her about her refugee status or the reason for her arrest.

## Timeline
- **Morning** - A.A. driving father's car to school in St. Cloud. At least two ICE vehicles pull her over; 4-5 agents approach.
- **Shortly after** - A.A. tells agents she is a minor and hands them her driver's license. Agents handcuff her in the street and force her to abandon the vehicle.
- **Later** - At Whipple Federal Building, agent tells A.A. to call her family. Agent threatens that if family comes, ICE will send entire family to Texas. Family decides not to come.
- **Evening** - ICE takes A.A. to an unnamed hotel. She spends the night in a room with two female agents. She has her own bed but not her own room.
- **Jan 14, midday** - Attorney Michelle Drake picks up A.A. from Whipple Federal Building.

## Official Accounts

### DHS/ICE Statement
DHS did not respond to the teen's allegations in court filings. MPR News emailed the agency for comment and to ask whether detaining unaccompanied children in hotels with federal agents is a regular practice. DHS requested to see the teen's declaration but had not responded as of publication.

### Attorney Statement
Attorney Michelle Drake stated that ICE weaponized the family's love for their daughter. "Refugees are people who have not been in this country very long, and they are escaping from authoritarian and totalitarian governments that do things like kidnap part of your family to get you to come down for an interrogation."

## Witness Accounts
From A.A.'s federal court declaration: She does not allege sexual or physical abuse by the agents. She states that at no time did anyone from the government try to interview her about her refugee status or the purported reason for her arrest. A.A. says she is now afraid to leave her house.
