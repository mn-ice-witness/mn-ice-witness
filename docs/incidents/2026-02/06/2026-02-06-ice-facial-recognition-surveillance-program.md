# ICE Deploys Facial Recognition Surveillance Program Against Citizen Observers in Minnesota

## Summary

Federal agents conducting Operation Metro Surge deployed a smartphone-based facial recognition application called Mobile Fortify to scan the faces of targets, bystanders, and citizen observers — connecting to federal databases containing more than 200 million photographs. A federal court filing, sworn declarations, and multiple investigations revealed that the technology was used not only to identify immigration targets but to build what border czar Tom Homan called a database to "make them famous" — a registry of U.S. citizens who observed, filmed, or protested ICE operations. At least seven named citizens were subjected to warrantless biometric scans or facial recognition-enabled retaliation. The ACLU filed a class-action lawsuit alleging forced facial scans had "become routine," and Congress introduced legislation to ban ICE and CBP from using the technology entirely.

## Mobile Fortify: The Technology

Mobile Fortify is a smartphone application developed by NEC Corporation under an approximately $23.9 million DHS contract. It enables agents to capture facial images and fingerprints using a standard government-issued cellphone camera, then compare them in real time against federal biometric databases.

According to internal ICE emails first obtained by 404 Media in June 2025: "The Mobile Fortify App empowers users with real-time biometric identity verification capabilities utilizing contactless fingerprints and facial images captured by the camera on an ICE issued cell phone without a secondary collection device."

CBP began using Mobile Fortify in early May 2025. ICE began on May 20, 2025. By the time Operation Metro Surge was launched, the app had been deployed more than 100,000 times in field operations nationally, according to a lawsuit filed by the State of Illinois and the City of Chicago.

### Databases Accessed

The app connects to federal biometric databases containing more than 200 million photographs:

- **IDENT / HART** (DHS Automated Biometric Identification System): 270+ million biometric records
- **CBP Traveler Verification Service** (passport and border crossing photos)
- **State Department visa and passport photo database**
- **FBI National Crime Information Center (NCIC)**
- A third category that remains **redacted** in DHS filings

DHS may retain photographs captured by the app for up to 15 years. There is no opt-out mechanism.

### Accuracy Concerns

NIST testing and civil liberties organizations have documented significantly higher error rates when facial recognition systems process darker-skinned individuals. 404 Media reported in January 2026 that a woman scanned by Mobile Fortify twice in the same interaction received two entirely different names — demonstrating accuracy problems with the system used to make detention decisions.

## The "Make Them Famous" Database

On January 15, 2026, border czar Tom Homan stated on Fox News: "We're going to create a database where those people that are arrested for interference... we're going to make them famous."

In at least one incident in Maine, an ICE agent told a legal observer after scanning her: "Because we have a nice little database. And now you're considered a domestic terrorist." DHS subsequently denied maintaining a "database of domestic terrorists."

The ACLU of Minnesota policy counsel John Boehler told KSTP that agents were "recording her face, documenting her identity for what the federal government has described as a database of observers," and that the practice was "increasingly common, at least in the Twin Cities." He said he expected ICE "to continue to use it more and more, both to build this database out, but also in an attempt to suppress the First Amendment activities of both people documenting and people who intend to document."

## Documented Incidents in Minnesota

Multiple named individuals were subjected to facial recognition scanning or recognition-enabled retaliation during Operation Metro Surge:

- [Nicole Cleland](#2026-01-10-nicole-cleland-global-entry-revoked) — 56-year-old Richfield observer addressed by name by an agent who disclosed facial recognition capability. Global Entry and TSA PreCheck revoked three days later.
- [Luis Martinez](#2026-01-27-luis-martinez-facial-recognition) — U.S. citizen stopped while driving to work. Agent held phone inches from his face and scanned using Mobile Fortify. Released only after producing passport.
- [Judy and Noah Levy](#2026-01-06-levy-observers-followed-home) — Observers whose faces and license plates were recorded, leading agents to their home.
- [Ryan Ecklund](#2026-01-14-ryan-ecklund-detained-shackled) — Woodbury observer detained approximately 10 hours after following ICE agents. Agents subsequently drove into his neighborhood.
- **Mubashir Khalif Hussen** — 20-year-old U.S. citizen of Somali descent. Despite asserting citizenship and offering his passport, agents forcibly scanned his face and then drove him seven miles outside Minneapolis and abandoned him in winter conditions. Named plaintiff in Hussen v. Noem class action.
- **Nekima Levy Armstrong** — Civil rights attorney arrested at anti-ICE demonstration. An agent visibly filmed her face during arrest. The White House subsequently posted an AI-altered image of her.
- **Steven Young** — Attorney who observed agents scanning occupants' faces during vehicle checkpoints on January 21.
- **Katie Henly** — Observer who reported agents photographing her license plate and face through her vehicle window.

NBC News also documented incidents outside Minnesota, including an agent in Aurora, Illinois asking "Can you do facial?" before holding a phone to a cyclist's face, and an agent in Portland, Maine photographing an observer who was recording them.

## Broader Surveillance Infrastructure

Facial recognition was one component of a larger surveillance apparatus deployed during Operation Metro Surge:

- **Clearview AI** — DHS maintains a $6 million contract with the company, whose database contains 50+ billion images scraped from the internet.
- **Palantir Technologies** — Nearly $30 million DHS contract for an AI-backed tracking system.
- **Flock Safety** — License plate reader technology deployed across the metro.
- **Thomson Reuters** — Database containing 20+ billion license plate scans.
- **Penlink Webloc** — Geofencing tool allowing agents to track all phones within a specific area.
- **Motorola Solutions** — Mobile license plate readers ordered by ICE.
- **Commercial phone location data** — Bulk purchase of cellphone location records.

Together with other surveillance data and systems, federal authorities can now monitor American cities at a scale that would have been difficult to imagine just a few years ago, advocates say.

DHS was piloting and deploying more than 100 artificial intelligence systems as of January 2026.

## FBI Expands Facial Recognition to Protest Investigations

According to Biometric Update, the FBI used facial recognition on still images extracted from publicly posted protest videos in at least one Minneapolis-related case. The identification appeared in a federal criminal complaint related to alleged property damage — demonstrating how protest footage posted by private citizens can be ingested into federal biometric systems without warrants or notice.

## Legal Challenges

### Hussen v. Noem (Class Action)

- **Case:** 0:26-cv-00324, U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota
- **Filed:** January 15, 2026
- **Judge:** Eric C. Tostrud
- **Plaintiffs:** Mubashir Khalif Hussen and others, plus a class of similarly situated individuals
- **Representation:** ACLU, ACLU of Minnesota, Covington & Burling LLP, Greene Espel PLLP, Robins Kaplan LLP
- **Core allegations:** Suspicionless stops, warrantless arrests, racial profiling, and forced facial scans that have "become routine"

- **Case:** 0:26-cv-00190, separate state-filed challenge

## Legislative Response

### ICE Out of Our Faces Act

Introduced February 5, 2026, by Sen. Ed Markey, Sen. Jeff Merkley, Sen. Ron Wyden, and Rep. Pramila Jayapal. Co-sponsored by Sen. Angela Alsobrooks and Sen. Bernie Sanders.

Key provisions:
- Ban ICE and CBP from acquiring or using facial recognition and biometric identification systems
- Require deletion of all biometric data already collected
- Allow individuals and state attorneys general to seek civil penalties

Endorsed by EFF, EPIC, Fight for the Future, ACLU, Leadership Conference Center for Civil Rights and Technology, Free Press Action, Access Now, and Human Rights First.

Sen. Markey had previously demanded ICE cease use of Mobile Fortify in September 2025, and sent a second unanswered letter in November 2025.

## DHS Response

DHS spokesperson: "Claims that Mobile Fortify violates the Fourth Amendment or compromises privacy are false. Mobile Fortify has not been blocked, restricted, or curtailed by the courts."

DHS on the database claims: "There is NO database of 'domestic terrorists' run by DHS."

DHS on surveillance generally: "Employing various forms of technology in support of investigations and law enforcement activities aids in the arrest of criminal gang members, child sex offenders, murderers, drug dealers, identity thieves and more, all while respecting civil liberties and privacy interests."

On February 2, DHS Secretary Noem announced body cameras would be deployed to "every officer in the field in Minneapolis" — effectively acknowledging the phone-based recording and scanning had been taking place.

## Expert Assessments

**Andrew Ferguson** (law professor, George Washington University) described the practice as "shocking," noting "such technology was neither ready for prime time, nor acceptable in a free society."

**Nathan Freed Wessler** (ACLU Speech, Privacy, and Technology Project) characterized the technology as "dangerous, totally unprecedented" and "blatantly illegal," emphasizing disproportionate impact on people of color due to higher error rates.

**India McKinney** (EFF Director of Federal Affairs): "Face surveillance in the hands of the government is a fundamentally harmful technology, even under strict regulations or if the technology was 100% accurate."

**Rachel Levinson-Waldman** (Brennan Center for Justice): "We are developing these technologies for immigrant enforcement... Are we also going to expand it or wield it against U.S. citizens who are engaging in entirely lawful or protest activity?"

**Dan Herman** (former CBP senior adviser, Center for American Progress): "They have access to a tremendous amount of trade, travel, immigration and screening data... there's a concern about the potential for abuse."

The U.S. Commission on Civil Rights warned in September 2024 that facial recognition raises concerns about accuracy, oversight, transparency, and discrimination — with no federal guidelines for use.
