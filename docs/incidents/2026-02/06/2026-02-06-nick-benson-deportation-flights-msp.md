# Volunteer Documents 42 Deportation Flights in January, Estimates 2,339 People Flown From Minnesota

## Summary
Nick Benson, a 41-year-old Burnsville man who works in commercial flight data analytics, has been tracking deportation flights leaving Minneapolis-St. Paul International Airport since the start of Operation Metro Surge. In January 2026, he documented 42 flights carrying an estimated 2,339 people — sometimes daily, sometimes twice a day. Using a 6-foot antenna on his roof, commercial flight tracking tools, and a telephoto camera at the airport, Benson counts shackled detainees as they are loaded onto charter Boeing 737s operated by Global Crossing Airlines (GlobalX). DHS does not release deportation flight data, making Benson's observations one of the only independent sources of quantitative information about how many people are being removed from Minnesota.

## How Benson Tracks Flights

Benson uses a 6-foot antenna attached to his roof in Burnsville to read data emitted by passing airplanes. When he identifies a likely deportation flight by its call sign or charter operator, he drives from Burnsville to MSP and positions himself at the Terminal 1 parking ramp with a digital camera, telephoto lens, and binoculars. He manually counts each individual as they are unloaded from DHS vans, shackled on the tarmac, and walked up the stairs to the aircraft.

Charter airlines were added to the FAA's privacy list in spring 2025, using special call signs instead of company identifiers. Benson noted: "It's the same mechanism that, like Taylor Swift and Elon Musk use to maintain privacy. It's unusual."

## What Benson Observes

Detainees arrive in DHS vans at the southern end of the airport. Authorities lay out shackles on the tarmac. People are unloaded from vans, placed in restraints at the hands and feet, and board charter Boeing 737s operated by GlobalX via stairs.

Benson observed one individual still wearing an Amazon work uniform. He observed elderly detainees needing assistance to board. Many pause at the top of the stairs and look around before entering the aircraft.

The vast majority of flights go to El Paso, Texas, where detainees are taken to Camp East Montana, a makeshift tent facility opened in August 2025.

**Savi Arvey, ICE Flight Monitor (Human Rights First):**
> "People are being put on these flights without due process, without the chance, oftentimes, to have their asylum claim heard before an immigration judge."

## DHS Response

DHS stated: "For operational security, ICE does not release information concerning deportation flight schedules."

The Metropolitan Airports Commission stated federal law prevents restricting airport access for law enforcement operations.
