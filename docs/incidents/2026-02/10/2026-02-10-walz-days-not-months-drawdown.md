# Walz Says ICE Drawdown Coming in "Days, Not Weeks and Months"

## Summary
At a press conference highlighting small business devastation, Governor Walz said he expects the federal immigration surge to end within days after conversations with border czar Tom Homan (Feb 9) and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles (Feb 10). Walz said he hoped Homan would announce "before Friday" that the operation was done, while noting he was "skeptical, but more hopeful than I have been." The city of Minneapolis estimated cumulative economic losses of $100 million from Operation Metro Surge.

## Key Quotes

### Governor Walz
"I spoke with Tom Homan, I spoke with Susie Wiles, this morning. We're very much in a trust but verify mode. But it's my expectation, and we will hear more from them, I think, in the next day or so, that we are talking days, not weeks and months of this occupation."

"It would be my hope that Mr. Homan goes out before Friday and announces that this thing is done, and they're bringing her down in days."

Walz said he was "skeptical, but more hopeful than I have been that they're going to wind this thing down."

## Context
At the time of Walz's statement, approximately 2,000 federal officers remained deployed in Minnesota — more than 13 times the pre-surge baseline of roughly 150 agents. On February 4, Homan had announced the withdrawal of 700 officers but conditioned a full drawdown on "cooperation" and "the decrease of the violence, the rhetoric and the attacks against ICE and Border Patrol." Restaurant operators at the press conference reported sales declines of 50% to 80% and severe staffing disruptions.
