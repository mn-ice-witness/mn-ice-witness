# Outstate Minnesota Cities Pass ICE Non-Cooperation Ordinances

## Summary
Multiple outstate Minnesota cities passed ordinances and resolutions restricting cooperation with federal immigration enforcement during the second week of February. Duluth unanimously prohibited city staff from assisting ICE. Mankato unanimously directed its city attorney to draft an ordinance requiring federal agents to unmask and wear body cameras. Red Wing approved a statement that its police will not assist in ICE operations. Moorhead debated similar motions but tabled them 5-3.

## City Actions

### Duluth (Feb 10) — Passed Unanimously
Unanimously passed a resolution prohibiting city staff from assisting federal immigration enforcement. A proposed eviction moratorium was tabled, causing community members in attendance to begin shouting. An anti-cooperation ordinance received a first reading and could go to vote Feb 23. Councilors Wendy Durrwachter and David Clanaugh proposed the eviction moratorium.

### Mankato (Feb 12) — Passed Unanimously
Unanimously instructed the city attorney to draft an ordinance requiring federal agents operating in Mankato to unmask, clearly identify their agency, and wear body cameras. Also unanimously directed staff to prepare an amicus brief supporting the Minneapolis/St. Paul/AG lawsuit against Operation Metro Surge.

### Red Wing (Feb 10) — Passed 5-2
Approved an official city statement affirming that Red Wing police will not assist or participate in ICE operations. Mayor Gary Iocco and five council members voted yes; Ron Goggin and Donald Kliewer dissented. Language about prohibiting use of public property for staging was removed after the city attorney advised they lacked jurisdiction.

### Moorhead (Feb 9-10) — Tabled 5-3
Council members brought motions to restrict ICE access to public property and examine police response to federal enforcement. The council voted 5-3 to table both motions and push them to a workshop.
