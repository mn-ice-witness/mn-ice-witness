# Fewer Than 500 ICE Agents Remain; Homan Promises 150 Within a Week; Observers Report Suburban Shift

## Summary
U.S. Reps. Ilhan Omar and Angie Craig toured the Whipple Federal Building on February 20 and confirmed that fewer than 500 ICE agents remain in Minnesota, down from approximately 3,000 at the peak of Operation Metro Surge. The facility's holding cells were empty. ICE St. Paul Field Office Director Sam Olson told the lawmakers that arrests had dropped to about 20 per day, deportation flights averaged two per week, and detainees were being held at four county jails. Border czar Tom Homan, who had returned to Washington, told CNN his goal was a "regular footprint" of 150 agents and that the force "should be back to regular footprint within a week." CNN reported the administration planned to replicate the Minneapolis model in other cities. Meanwhile, Sahan Journal and community observers reported that ICE had not left the suburbs — agents had shifted to stealthier tactics in communities like Eden Prairie, Columbia Heights, and Fridley.
