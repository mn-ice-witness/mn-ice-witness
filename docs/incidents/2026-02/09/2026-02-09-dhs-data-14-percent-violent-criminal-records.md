# Internal DHS Data: Less Than 14% of ICE Arrests Had Violent Criminal Records

## Summary
An internal Department of Homeland Security document obtained by CBS News revealed that less than 14% of the nearly 393,000 people arrested by ICE in President Trump's first year back in office had charges or convictions for violent criminal offenses. Nearly 40% had no criminal record at all and were detained only for civil immigration violations. Less than 2% had homicide or sexual assault charges, and less than 2% had gang affiliations. The data directly contradicts the administration's repeated assertions that its deportation operations primarily target "the worst of the worst." In Minnesota, U.S. District Judge Jerry Blackwell had independently observed that "the overwhelming majority" of ICE cases brought before him involved people lawfully present in the state.

## The Data

The internal DHS document covers all ICE arrests from January 21, 2025 through January 31, 2026 — approximately 393,000 total arrests.

### Breakdown by Criminal Status

| Category | Number | Percentage |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Prior criminal charges or convictions ("criminal aliens") | ~229,000 | 58% |
| Civil immigration charges only (no criminal record) | ~153,000 | 39% |
| New criminal allegations (e.g., interfering with operations) | ~11,000 | 3% |

The document lists the most serious charge or conviction at the time of arrest:

| Weapons offenses | 6,146 | 1.6% |
| Sexual assault | 5,365 | 1.4% |

Combined violent crime categories (homicide, sexual assault, robbery, kidnapping, arson, assault, weapons) represent approximately 13.9% of all arrests.

### Comparison to Biden Administration
ICE made more than triple the number of administrative arrests over the past year compared to fiscal year 2024 (393,000 vs. 113,000). However, the percentage of arrests involving people with criminal histories dropped from 72% under Biden to 58% under Trump.

## Official Accounts

### DHS Response
DHS spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin pushed back on the CBS report the same day, arguing that categorizing offenses as "non-violent" was misleading: "Drug trafficking, distribution of child pornography, burglary, fraud, DUI, embezzlement, solicitation of a minor, human smuggling are all categorized as 'nonviolent crimes.'" DHS simultaneously published a press release on WOW.DHS.GOV highlighting specific arrests of individuals with violent criminal histories, timed to coincide with Super Bowl Sunday.

### Homan Warning
NBC News reported the same day that Border Czar Tom Homan had previously warned that immigration operations should be targeted to "keep the faith of the American people," suggesting awareness within the administration that sweeping enforcement could erode public support.

## Minnesota Connection

U.S. District Judge Jerry Blackwell stated during a February hearing that "the overwhelming majority of the hundreds seen by this court have been found to be lawfully present" — an observation from the bench corroborating the national data at the local level. The CBS report specifically noted that Border Patrol agents had been deployed to Minneapolis as part of aggressive arrest operations, and that the DHS arrest data did not include Border Patrol arrests, meaning the overall figures may undercount operations in cities like Minneapolis.

A CBS News poll found American support for Trump's deportation efforts had fallen to 46%, down from 59% at the start of his second term, with over 60% saying agents were being "too tough."
