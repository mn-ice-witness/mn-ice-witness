# Reports of ICE Agents Using Disguises, Fake License Plates, and Ruse Tactics Across Minnesota

## Summary

An Associated Press investigation published February 8 documented a pattern of deceptive tactics used by federal agents during Operation Metro Surge, including disguising themselves as construction workers, using fake business decals on rental vehicles, swapping or obscuring license plates, and impersonating delivery drivers and anti-ICE observers. A separate Star Tribune investigation of 100+ license plates on ICE vehicles found nearly 60% were not registered with the state of Minnesota. Governor Walz called the plate-swapping "criminal activity" that violates Minnesota law. These revelations formalized what communities, observers, and legal advocates had been reporting for weeks — that agents were using an escalating range of ruse tactics to avoid the citizen observer networks that had become a defining feature of Operation Metro Surge.

## Disguises and Impersonation

### Construction Worker Outfits

Luis Ramirez, 31, a Shakopee restaurant owner, noticed men dressed as utility workers outside his family's Mexican restaurant for days — wearing high-visibility vests and "spotless white hard hats, even while parked." The vehicle was a rented Nissan with fake business decals advertising a Wisconsin-based electrician that did not exist. When the vehicle returned on February 4, Ramirez filmed a confrontation with two men who hid their faces and appeared to be wearing heavy tactical gear beneath their yellow vests. A DHS spokesperson later confirmed one of the men was an ICE agent conducting surveillance.

Ramirez is the same U.S. citizen who was [tackled by ICE at a Shakopee construction site](#2026-01-07-luis-ramirez-orozco-shakopee) on January 7, suffering bruised ribs and a concussion.

Jose Alvillar, a lead organizer for Unidos MN, reported that agents have "repeatedly shown up to construction sites dressed as workers." He added: "Construction workers are good at identifying who is a real construction worker and who is dressing up as one."

### Other Reported Disguises

According to Lee Steadman of a constitutional observer network, agents have also been spotted disguised as Uber drivers, electrical workers, and school observers. The AP reported that legal observers and officials had received a growing number of reports of agents impersonating delivery drivers and anti-ICE activists, though the AP noted "not all of those incidents have been verified."

Activists monitoring the Whipple Federal Building told the AP they had seen agents leaving in vehicles with stuffed animals on dashboards, Mexican flag decals on bumpers, and pickups with lumber or tools in their beds.

## License Plate Deception

### Star Tribune Investigation

A Star Tribune review of more than 100 Minnesota license plates on ICE vehicles found:

- **Nearly 60%** were not registered with the state of Minnesota
- **11%** had irregularities including expired tabs from different vehicles or plates registered to a nonexistent business
- **25%** of vehicles examined were rentals
- A Jeep Cherokee was displaying plates last registered in 2014 to a **Porsche 911 Turbo**
- Another Jeep was displaying plates registered to a **Ferrari** with 2019 tabs
- One license plate was observed on **four different vehicles**
- Two vehicles with **identical plates were photographed driving together**, one a few feet behind the other
- The Chevy Tahoe that federal agent Jonathan Ross traveled in the day he fatally shot Renee Good had **unregistered plates**
- Agents also obscured plates with mud, snow (sometimes fake snow), and illegal plate covers, or drove without plates entirely

### Attempted License Plate Purchase

On January 13, antiques dealer Candice Metrailer received a call from a man asking if her south Minneapolis shop sold license plates. Minutes later, two men in street clothes entered and browsed her collection of vintage plates. "One of them says, 'Hey, do you have any recent ones?' Immediately, an alarm bell went off in my head." She refused the sale. A Ford Explorer with blacked-out windows idled nearby; she memorized the plate and cross-referenced it with an activist database that traced it to the Whipple Federal Building. She reported the incident to the Minnesota Attorney General.

### Governor Walz Response

Governor Walz called the plate-swapping "criminal activity," stating: "These guys are doing what criminals do: They're putting license plates on vehicles they're not registered to." Minnesota's Director of Driver and Vehicle Services, Pong Xiong, sent a letter to DHS Secretary Kristi Noem warning that "DVS has also received reports that identical license plates have been observed on the rear of two separate DHS vehicles" and that "this conduct violates Minnesota law and will not be tolerated."

## Luring and Trickery

Federal agents also employed ruse tactics to lure individuals out of safe locations:

- **Fake car accident:** On January 7, a teacher at Un Mundo Nuevo daycare in Burnsville was [tricked by agents who falsely claimed someone had hit her car](#2026-01-07-un-mundo-nuevo-teacher-tricked). When she stepped outside, ICE detained her for 10 minutes.
- **Fake food flyers:** On January 22, Anne Sullivan STEAM School in Minneapolis [warned families](#2026-01-22-sullivan-elementary-ice-food-flyers) that ICE was "sending out flyers offering food support to families" and urged parents not to respond. The school's warning was confirmed as authentic, but no actual flyer has been produced or independently verified as coming from ICE.
- **Child used as leverage:** On January 20, five-year-old [Liam Ramos was detained](#2026-01-20-liam-ramos-detained) in his driveway returning from preschool. School officials and witnesses said agents refused to leave the child with another adult, using him to lure his father. DHS disputed this characterization.
- **False pretense for arrest:** ICE agents told a Venezuelan refugee family "they just needed to verify the fingerprints for our oldest son, but he would not be detained." The son was then arrested and driven away.

## Surveillance Against Observers

The deceptive tactics extended to targeting the citizen observer networks themselves:

- **Facial recognition:** Agents used a smartphone app called "Mobile Fortify" to scan observers' faces, linking into a database of over 200 million photos. Observer [Nicole Cleland](#2026-01-10-nicole-cleland-global-entry-revoked) was addressed by name by an agent who informed her of the facial recognition capability; her Global Entry and TSA PreCheck were revoked three days later.
- **License plate tracking:** Observer [Judy Levy](#2026-01-06-levy-observers-followed-home) was greeted by name by an ICE agent who had run her plates. She responded: "I go by Judy. Nobody calls me Judith, unless they've just run my plates." The agents then drove to the street where the Levys live.
- **Following observers home:** Multiple observers reported being tracked to their neighborhoods, including Ryan Ecklund in Woodbury: "They drove into my neighborhood and into my cul-de-sac, where I live."

## Expert and Legal Assessments

**Naureen Shah, ACLU Director of Immigration Advocacy:** "If you have people afraid that the electrical worker outside their house might be ICE, you're inviting public distrust and confusion on a much more dangerous level. This is what you do if you're trying to control a populace, not trying to do routine, professional law enforcement."

**Scott Mechkowski, former deputy director of ICE enforcement in New York:** Said that in nearly three decades in immigration enforcement, he hadn't seen ICE agents disguising themselves as uniformed workers to make arrests. The operations reported in Minnesota represent "a more extreme degree than we've seen in the past."

The ACLU of Minnesota filed a class-action suit alleging that "forced facial scans have become routine." The [Immigrant Defense Project](https://www.immigrantdefenseproject.org/ice-ruses/) has documented ICE ruse tactics nationally since 2017, but noted the Minnesota operations represent a significant escalation.
