# Homan Declares Operation Metro Surge Over

## Summary
At a press conference at the Whipple Federal Building at Fort Snelling, border czar Tom Homan announced the conclusion of Operation Metro Surge, saying President Trump concurred with his proposal to end the 10-week operation. Homan declared Minnesota "now less of a sanctuary state for criminals," cited over 4,000 arrests, and said a significant drawdown was already underway. He warned that mass deportations would continue nationwide and that churches and schools remained "not off the table." A small contingent of personnel will remain to complete fraud investigations and criminal cases against "agitators."

## Key Quotes

### Tom Homan
"I have proposed, and President Trump has concurred, that this surge operation conclude."

"As a result of our efforts here, Minnesota is now less of a sanctuary state for criminals."

"This community is safer because we arrested 4,000 — over 4,000 — illegal aliens here, many of them public safety threats, not all of them."

"I don't want to see any more bloodshed. I pray every night for the safety of law enforcement personnel and the safety of those in the community, whether you're here legally or illegally."

"For those who say we are backing down from immigration enforcement or the promise of mass deportations you are simply wrong. President Trump made a promise of mass deportations and that's what this country is going to get."

"If you're in this country illegally, you're not off the table."

On churches and schools: ICE agents "had not gone into churches or schools" but "those locations are not off the table."

### Governor Walz
"The long road to recovery starts now. The impact on our economy, our schools, and people's lives won't be reversed overnight."

### Mayor Frey
"They thought they could break us, but a love for our neighbors and a resolve to endure can outlast an occupation."

## Operation Statistics (per DHS)
- **Total arrests:** Over 4,000
- **Arrests for "impeding law enforcement":** Over 200
- **Peak deployment:** ~3,000 federal officers (ICE, Border Patrol, CBP)
- **Pre-surge baseline:** ~80-150 ICE officers in Minnesota
- **Operation duration:** ~10 weeks (late November 2025 to February 12, 2026)

## What Remains After Drawdown
- Small footprint of personnel to close out operations and transition command to the local field office
- Federal personnel conducting criminal investigations into "agitators"
- Personnel assigned to fraud investigations
- Homan said he would "remain on the ground for a little longer" to oversee the drawdown

## Context
The announcement came the same morning that top Minnesota officials testified at a U.S. Senate hearing on immigration enforcement. Attorney General Keith Ellison told senators that Operation Metro Surge caused "real harm" and was about "retribution and reckoning." NBC News reported Trump's approval rating had declined to 39%, with 49% of adults strongly disapproving of his immigration handling — up from 38% the previous summer. Two U.S. citizens — [Renee Good](/entry/2026-01-07-renee-good-shooting) and [Alex Pretti](/entry/2026-01-24-alex-pretti-shooting) — were fatally shot by federal agents during the operation.
