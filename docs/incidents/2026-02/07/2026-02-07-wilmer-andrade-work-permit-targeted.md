# ICE Targets Man with Valid Work Permit and Pending Crime Victim Visa

## Summary
ICE agents knocked on Wilmer Andrade's door in the Twin Cities suburbs seeking his arrest, despite Andrade holding a valid work permit and a pending U visa application as a crime victim. Andrade, who was beaten and robbed in Minneapolis five years ago and has no criminal history in Minnesota, refused to open the door and was not taken into custody. The incident is part of a pattern of Operation Metro Surge increasingly targeting immigrants with active work authorization and pending legal cases.

## What Happened
On Saturday, February 7, ICE agents arrived at Wilmer Andrade's home in the Twin Cities suburbs and knocked on his door, claiming a warrant for his arrest. Andrade has held a valid work permit for two years and has a pending U visa application — a visa category reserved for victims of serious crimes who assist law enforcement. Andrade qualified after being beaten and robbed in Minneapolis five years ago.

A review of Minnesota court records shows no criminal history. He has no final order of removal.

Andrade refused to open the door and was not detained.

## Context
The Sahan Journal investigation found that ICE and Border Patrol agents under Operation Metro Surge are increasingly targeting people with valid work permits and pending visa or asylum applications — people who are lawfully present and cooperating with the immigration system. The U visa program was created by Congress specifically to encourage crime victims to work with law enforcement without fear of deportation.
