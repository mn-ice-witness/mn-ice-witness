# 11 Federal Agents Smash Into Apartment Building at 6 a.m. to Arrest Man for Anti-ICE Social Media Posts

## Summary
At approximately 6 a.m. on February 5, 2026, at least 11 Homeland Security Investigations officers in camouflage and masks broke through the security window of Eat Street Flats — a residential apartment building in Minneapolis's Whittier neighborhood — to arrest 37-year-old Kyle Wagner. Rather than contact the building's property manager or use the front door, agents smashed the glass beside the security entrance, then broke down Wagner's apartment door upstairs. Wagner was charged with cyberstalking and interstate communications out of the U.S. Attorney's Office in Eastern Michigan for allegedly doxxing a pro-ICE individual and making threatening social media posts against ICE agents. A First Amendment attorney told Sahan Journal that much of the cited speech, viewed in isolation, is constitutionally protected hypothetical speech. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem called Wagner "a self-identified Antifa domestic terrorist." The broken security window left the building's entrance unsecured for all residents. A contractor who boarded up the building said the apartment unit was a mess with things "scattered everywhere" and that agents "didn't care." Another building occupant reported that agents left Wagner's dog behind; a neighbor took in the animal.

## Building Impact
The raid left the security entrance of a multi-unit residential building unsecured. Jose Piedra, a contractor tasked with boarding up the building, told Sahan Journal the apartment unit upstairs was a mess with things "scattered everywhere." He said of federal officers: "You can see they didn't care." A person who works in the building reported on Reddit that agents "took a guy out of his home and left their dog to fend for themselves." A Reddit commenter also noted that the building had a posted notice stating that if ICE needed access, they could call the landlord — but agents smashed the window instead.

## First Amendment Analysis
Brennen VanderVeen, a First Amendment attorney with the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression (FIRE), told Sahan Journal that viewed in isolation, much of the speech cited in the complaint is constitutionally protected. He cited the Vietnam War-era Supreme Court case where a man just drafted said if given a gun, "the first man I want to get in my sights" was then-President Lyndon Johnson — upheld as protected hypothetical speech.

VanderVeen noted that doxxing is not a specific legal category and is generally legal — "If you hack into a government database and then you release the information, that's going to be a problem. But you are just aware of information and you're sharing it, that's not generally going to be a crime." He said the government appeared to be using Wagner's previous posts and photos of him "tattooed up with an angry face" to "paint a particular picture."

## Context
The raid came one day after federal officials announced they were withdrawing 700 immigration officers from Operation Metro Surge, still leaving about 2,000 in the area. The criminal complaint was filed out of the Eastern District of Michigan, not Minnesota, even though Wagner lives in Minneapolis. The White House immediately publicized the arrest, with the press secretary announcing it at a Washington press conference. The characterization of Antifa as a "terrorist organization" by the Attorney General has no legal basis — no U.S. law designates domestic groups as terrorist organizations.
