# Leaked CBP Inventory Reveals 35,765 Munitions Stockpiled for Operation Metro Surge

## Summary
A leaked Customs and Border Protection document titled "CBP Table of Munitions Used in Operation Metro Surge" revealed that 35,765 munitions were stockpiled on-site in Minneapolis by mid-January 2026, the majority intended for crowd control rather than immigration enforcement. The inventory included over 1,100 riot control gas grenades and thousands of less-than-lethal munitions rounds. A homeland security official shown the list said: "Fucking overkill. I've never seen this amount of shit being used." The leak was published on the same day that border czar Tom Homan announced the withdrawal of 700 federal agents from Minnesota.

## The Leaked Document

The inventory, produced by CBP, listed over a dozen different types of crowd-control munitions documented as "on-site" in Minneapolis. The weapons arrived approximately two weeks before publication — roughly mid-to-late January 2026. Confirmed types include:

- **Tear gas** (CS gas)
- **Stun grenades / flashbangs**
- **Pepper spray** (OC)
- **Riot control gas grenades** — over 1,100
- **Rubber Ball Blast Grenades** (rubber balls the size of large marbles burst in all directions)
- **Triple-Chaser Separating Canisters** (split into separate canisters landing ~20 feet apart)
- **40mm foam/rubber rounds**
- **Thousands of less-than-lethal munitions rounds**

All manufactured by Defense Technology.

As Klippenstein wrote, "the majority of weapons shipped to Minneapolis are intended to battle protesters, not to facilitate immigration enforcement."

## Official Response

DHS did not publicly confirm or deny the document's authenticity. No official response to the leak was found.

The same day the inventory was published, Homan announced the withdrawal of 700 agents from Minnesota "effective immediately." Approximately 2,000 federal personnel remained — 13 times the agencies' normal footprint.

## Verification

The leaked inventory was subsequently cited in Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison's expedited discovery motion in the state's lawsuit against ICE, referencing "recently leaked DHS internal memos" that "raise factual questions about Defendants' policies, practices, training, or rules of engagement with Minnesota residents."

Ken Klippenstein is a respected independent journalist (formerly of The Intercept). He independently showed the document to a homeland security official who reacted to its contents. No outlet has reported the document is fabricated.
