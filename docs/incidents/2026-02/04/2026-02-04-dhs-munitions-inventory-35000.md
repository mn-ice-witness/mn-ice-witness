# Leaked CBP Inventory Reveals 35,765 Munitions Stockpiled for Operation Metro Surge

## Summary
A leaked Customs and Border Protection document titled "CBP Table of Munitions Used in Operation Metro Surge" revealed that 35,765 munitions were stockpiled on-site in Minneapolis by mid-January 2026, the majority intended for crowd control rather than immigration enforcement. The inventory included over 1,100 riot control gas grenades and thousands of less-than-lethal munitions rounds. A homeland security official shown the list said: "Fucking overkill. I've never seen this amount of shit being used." The leak was published on the same day that border czar Tom Homan announced the withdrawal of 700 federal agents from Minnesota.

## Sources
1. Ken Klippenstein / Substack (Feb 4, 2026): [In ICE's War, the Public Is Winning](https://www.kenklippenstein.com/p/in-ices-war-the-public-is-winning)
2. Raw Story (Feb 4, 2026): [Leaked munitions list for Trump's Minnesota crackdown horrifies DHS official: 'Overkill'](https://www.rawstory.com/trump-ice-2675090158/)

## The Leaked Document

The inventory, produced by CBP, listed over a dozen different types of crowd-control munitions documented as "on-site" in Minneapolis. The weapons arrived approximately two weeks before publication — roughly mid-to-late January 2026. The article specifically names:

- **Tear gas** (CS gas)
- **Stun grenades / flashbangs**
- **Pepper spray** (OC)
- **Riot control gas grenades** — over 1,100
- **Thousands of less-than-lethal munitions rounds**

As Klippenstein wrote, the list "shows that the majority of weapons shipped to Minneapolis (and which arrived two weeks ago) are intended to battle protesters." He added: "These weapons are intended for protesters, not to facilitate immigration enforcement."

## Official Response

DHS did not publicly confirm or deny the document's authenticity. No official response to the leak was found.

The same day the inventory was published, Homan announced the withdrawal of 700 agents from Minnesota "effective immediately." Approximately 2,000 federal personnel remained.

## Verification

Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison's expedited discovery motion in the state's lawsuit against ICE referenced "recently leaked DHS internal memos" that "raise factual questions about Defendants' policies, practices, training, or rules of engagement with Minnesota residents." The motion sought documents related to the decision to launch Operation Metro Surge and the policies governing agents' conduct.

Ken Klippenstein is a respected independent journalist (formerly of The Intercept). He independently showed the document to a homeland security official who reacted to its contents. No outlet has reported the document is fabricated.
