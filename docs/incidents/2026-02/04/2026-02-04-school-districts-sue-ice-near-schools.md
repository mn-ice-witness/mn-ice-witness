# Minnesota School Districts and Teachers Union Sue to Restore Schools as Protected Areas

## Summary
Fridley Public Schools, Duluth Public Schools, and Education Minnesota (the statewide teachers union representing 84,000 educators) filed a federal lawsuit on February 4, 2026, seeking to bar DHS and ICE from conducting immigration enforcement near public schools. The complaint documents ICE staging operations in school parking lots, following superintendents and school board members, pulling over school transportation vans with students aboard, and causing attendance to drop by as much as one-third in affected districts. The suit argues DHS rescinded decades of "sensitive locations" protections without following required rulemaking procedures under the Administrative Procedure Act.

## Case Details

- **Case:** Fridley Public School District, ISD 14 v. Noem, Case No. 0:26-cv-01023 (LMP/LIB)
- **Court:** U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota
- **Filed:** February 4, 2026
- **Judge:** Laura M. Provinzino; Magistrate Judge Leo I. Brisbois
- **Plaintiffs:** Fridley Public Schools (ISD 14), Duluth Public Schools (ISD 709), Education Minnesota
- **Defendants:** Kristi Noem (DHS Secretary), Todd Lyons (Acting ICE Director), Marcos Charles (Acting ERO Director), David Easterwood (St. Paul Field Office), Rodney Scott (CBP Commissioner), Gregory Bovino (Border Patrol Commander), Thomas Homan (Border Czar), DHS, ICE, and CBP
- **Legal basis:** Administrative Procedure Act — DHS rescinded the decades-old "sensitive locations" policy without required notice-and-comment rulemaking
- **Representation:** Zimmerman Reed LLP, Nilan Johnson Lewis PA, Democracy Forward

## Relief Requested

1. Court order barring DHS and ICE from conducting immigration enforcement in or near public school grounds
2. Reinstatement of the federal government's "protected areas" policy designating schools as sensitive locations
3. Prohibition of immigration operations within 1,000 feet of school property and bus stops absent a judicial warrant or exigent circumstances

## Attendance Impact

### Fridley Public Schools
- Attendance dropped nearly one-third since Operation Metro Surge began
- Over 400 families enrolled in virtual learning
- Schools closed January 9 and January 16 due to safety concerns

### Columbia Heights
- Average 130 absences daily from a student body of 570 (majority Hispanic)
- Approximately 300 students (25% of student body) opted for online learning
- Sports teams forfeited games due to player fears

### St. Paul Public Schools
- 50% of Spanish-speaking students absent on January 9
- 25% of Somali students absent on January 9

### Duluth Public Schools
- Increased student absences, particularly among immigrant community families
- BIPOC employees began carrying passports to work

## Legal Context

The complaint traces federal guidance discouraging school enforcement back to 1993. Subsequent policies in 2007, 2008, 2011, 2013, and 2021 reinforced schools as sensitive or protected locations, requiring headquarters or supervisor approval before enforcement at such sites. The new policy relies only on individual agent "common sense" rather than supervisor approval.

This case is separate from the State of Minnesota v. Noem lawsuit (0:26-cv-00190), filed January 12, 2026, which is before Judge Katherine M. Menendez.
